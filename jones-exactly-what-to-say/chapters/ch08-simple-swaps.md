# Chapter 8: Simple Swaps

## Core Idea
Changing a couple of words moves control of the conversation. Two swaps every practitioner should install permanently: how you invite questions after a pitch, and how you ask for contact info.

## Swap 1: Inviting Questions
> **Don't say:** "Do you have any questions?"
> **Say:** **"What questions do you have for me?"**

**Why:**
- "Do you have any questions?" suggests they *should*, and if they don't they feel stupid — so they say "let me think about it" and leave.
- "What questions do you have for me?" presupposes they have some. If they say "none," they're signaling they've already decided — which is your cue to ask for the decision.
- Either you get their specific blockers or you get a close signal.

## Swap 2: Asking for Contact Info
> **Don't say:** "Can I have your phone number?"
> **Say:** **"What's the best number to contact you at?"**

**Why:**
- "Can I have…?" is a permission request → yes/no → feels invasive → resistance.
- "What's the best…?" presupposes they'll give it — the only choice is which one.

## Why Both Work
Both swaps convert a yes/no question into a wh-question. Yes/no invites refusal; wh-questions invite information. The move is universal — apply it anywhere you catch yourself asking permission.

## When to Use
- **Swap 1:** at the end of every presentation, pitch, or recommendation.
- **Swap 2:** when asking for email, phone, address, meeting time, calendar hold.
- Generally: whenever you hear yourself start with "Do you…" or "Can I…" and realize you want actual information.

## How
1. Catch the permission-framed question before it leaves your mouth.
2. Rewrite as a wh-question that presupposes compliance.
3. Deliver matter-of-factly — the confidence is part of the lever.

## More Examples to Install
- "Can you send me the file?" → "What's the easiest way for you to get me the file?"
- "Do you want to meet this week?" → "What day this week works best for us to meet?"
- "Can I follow up next Tuesday?" → "What time Tuesday is easiest for me to call you?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Switching back under social pressure** ("Just checking — do you maybe have any questions?"). Kills the frame.
- **Over-politeness that re-introduces permission** ("What questions, if any, might you possibly have?"). Keep it clean.

## Key Takeaways
1. Permission-framed questions hand control to the other person. Wh-framed questions keep control.
2. These swaps are not phrases to deploy — they're defaults to install permanently.
3. If you catch yourself opening with "Do you…" or "Can I…," reflex: swap.
