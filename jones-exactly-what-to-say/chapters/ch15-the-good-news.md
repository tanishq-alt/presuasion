# Chapter 15: The Good News

## Core Idea
Labeling. The moment you apply a label to a situation, it sticks. "The good news is…" reframes any negative into a positive, and the listener has to adopt the new label to respond.

## The Magic Words
> **"The good news is…"**
> Companion phrase: **"That's great…"** (for receiving excuses)

## Why It Works
- **Labels stick.** Call something good news and the brain treats it that way — arguing with it means arguing with a positive, which most people won't do.
- **Interrupts the blame/pity loop.** Negative conversations spiral; this phrase is the circuit-breaker.
- **Flips excuses into progress.** "I couldn't do it because of X" — "That's great, you've just found another way that doesn't work." (Edison framing.)

## When to Use
- Prospect expressing doubt about themselves ("I don't have the skills…").
- Resistance to change ("I'm not sure, I've always done it this way").
- Any excuse or objection where you'd otherwise argue.

## How ("The good news is…")
1. Listen for the self-doubt or negative frame.
2. Preface your response with "The good news is…"
3. Follow with a specific, credible positive — not a generic reassurance.

## How ("That's great…")
1. Receive the excuse without pushback.
2. Say "That's great" (calmly, not sarcastically).
3. Reframe their obstacle as information or progress.

## Worked Examples
- Them: "I'm not sure I can do this." → "The good news is we have dozens of people who were in exactly the same situation when they first started, and they've gone on to be successful and are here to support you."
- Them: "I don't think I have the skills." → "The good news is we have comprehensive training you can complete at your own pace to give you all the skills you need."
- Them: "What I'm doing now isn't working but I'm reluctant to change." → "The good news is you already know what you're doing now isn't working, so what is the harm in trying this?"
- Them: "I couldn't do it because of X." → "That's great — you've just found out another way that doesn't work."

## Anti-patterns
- **Sarcastic delivery of "that's great."** Flips the move from reframe to mockery.
- **Fake good news.** If the follow-up positive is hollow, the frame collapses.
- **Overused.** Twice in a conversation is the limit before it sounds scripted.

## Key Takeaways
1. Labels applied in conversation are hard to take off — use the mechanism deliberately.
2. "That's great" is the circuit-breaker for excuse spirals.
3. Specific positives beat generic reassurance. Always.
