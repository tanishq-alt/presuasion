# Chapter 11: I Bet You're a Bit Like Me

## Core Idea
Strangers follow the path of least resistance in conversation. Prefacing a reasonable statement with "I bet you're a bit like me…" quietly loads it so they agree — and now you have on-record evidence to use at close.

## The Magic Words
> **"I bet you're a bit like me — [reasonable claim about them]."**

## Why It Works
- **Social compliance with strangers** is high when the ask is just a nod.
- **Pre-committed agreement.** They've signed off on the premise; it's harder for them to contradict later.
- **Blocks future objections before they form** — if they've agreed they're busy and ambitious, "I don't have time" becomes much harder to use against you.

## When to Use
- Early in conversations with new prospects or strangers.
- To pre-neutralize objections you can anticipate (time, risk, change-aversion).
- To gather on-record premises your close will lean on.

## How
1. Pick a claim that is **reasonable and flattering** — they'll want to agree.
2. Deliver with eye contact; nod gently while saying it (mirror behavior cues them to nod back).
3. Watch for the nod. The nod *is* the agreement — you don't need them to say it.
4. Later, when the objection appears, reference the earlier agreement: "Earlier you agreed you're always juggling to get things done…"

## Worked Examples
- "I bet you're a bit like me: you enjoy working hard now, knowing it will pay dividends in the future."
- "I bet you're a bit like me: you hate watching trashy TV in the evening and would rather work on something beneficial."
- "I bet you're a bit like me: you're a busy person who's always juggling to get everything done."

## Anti-patterns
- **Unreasonable or oddly specific claims.** "I bet you're a bit like me: you only drink pour-over coffee from Ethiopian single-origin beans." They can't agree without feeling weird.
- **Negative framing** ("I bet you're a bit like me: you hate your job"). People won't admit this to a stranger.
- **Forgetting the premise later.** Its whole value is the callback at close — bank it.

## Key Takeaways
1. Compliance is cheapest with strangers under low stakes — exploit this early.
2. Pre-loaded agreements become ammunition to dissolve later objections.
3. A nod counts as "yes" — don't demand verbal confirmation.
