# Chapter 8: Persuasive Geographies — All the Right Places, All the Right Traces

## Core Idea
There is a geography of influence. Places — physical and psychological — carry pre-loaded associations. We can **send ourselves in desired directions** by locating to environments pre-fit with cues for our goals. We can also move others into such environments. And we can activate the **inner geography** — the units of experience already lying in wait inside people — by directing attention there.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **External self-persuasive geography** — the physical space around you and its cue load. Where you work determines what you think about by default.
- **Internal self-persuasive geography** — the units of experience (attitudes, memories, capacities) already in you; attention activates whichever one it touches.
- **Attention management as a happiness lever** (Carstensen, Lyubomirsky) — the elderly feel *happier* despite physical decline because they've prioritized emotional contentment and route attention to positive memories, faces, upsides.
- **Stereotype threat** — reminding someone of a negative in-group stereotype activates it and degrades performance. Reminding someone of a *positive* in-group stereotype activates *that* and improves performance. Same person, same test.

## External Geography — Choose Your Workspace

**Cialdini's two desks.** Writing his first popular book, he had a university office overlooking academic buildings, surrounded by journals and colleagues' books. He had a home desk overlooking pedestrians, surrounded by magazines and newspapers. A month in, he realized: the home writing was *miles* better — more suited to the general audience. First line at the university desk: *"My academic subdiscipline, experimental social psychology, has as a principal domain the study of the social influence process."* First line revised at home: *"I can admit it freely now: all my life I've been a patsy."*

**The incentive-program consultant.** Reported that programs designed in glass-walled central conference rooms (with constant low-level sight of the client's employees) turned out better than programs designed in closed rooms. Her team couldn't control room assignments — so they printed enlarged action photos of client employees and leaned them around whatever room they got. Clients loved the "personalized touch." The team was doing pre-suasion *on themselves.*

**Lesson:** when you can, choose the environment whose default cues align with the output you want. When you can't, import the cues.

## Internal Geography — Activate What's Already in You

**Contagion and the medical-student effect.** 70–80% of medical students develop symptoms of whatever disease they're studying — because attention to a lung spot, an appendix location, a skin area, activates sensations that were always ambiently present. Cough spreads through theaters; itch spreads through dermatology lectures; cancer-rate anxiety appears after false statistics are reported and only partially fades when retracted. Austrian spider panic: hospitals flooded with people certain they'd been bitten (4,000% more wrong than right).

**Walton (1908):** "The knowledge that pneumonia produces pain in a certain spot leads to a concentration of attention upon that region, which causes any sensation there to give alarm."

**The lever runs both ways.** Attention activates whatever you direct it toward — pain, cough, nausea, OR happiness, confidence, strength. Pre-suaders can self-direct.

## The Happiness Paradox and Three Levers
The elderly feel happier than they did younger despite real losses. Carstensen: because they've decided they *don't have time* for negativity — "time" meaning remaining life, not daily availability. They route attention to positive memories, happy faces, upsides. The seniors with the best attention-management skills show the greatest mood enhancement.

Lyubomirsky's top three attention-based happiness activities:
1. **Count blessings/gratitudes at the start of every day** — then write them down to get concentrated time with them.
2. **Cultivate optimism** — choose in advance to look on the bright side of situations and futures.
3. **Negate the negative** — deliberately limit time dwelling on problems or unhealthy social comparisons.

"You can make yourself happier like you can make yourself lose weight. But you have to put in the effort every day." Younger people's primary goals (learning, striving, achieving) require openness to discomfort, so forced attention to positives would be maladaptive full-time — but the activities above are still available for breaking negative loops.

## Alan Chaikin's GRE Trick
Cialdini's grad-school classmate scored top 1% on all four GRE sections. He wasn't smarter in general — he was smarter at *pre-suading himself before the exam.* While other students crammed uncertain material in the last ten minutes, Alan spent that time *calming fears and building confidence by reviewing past successes and genuine strengths.* "You can't think straight when you're scared, plus, you're much more persistent when you're confident." Tactical intelligence: turning general knowledge (fear hurts; confidence helps) into pre-suasive application.

## Moving Others — The STEM School Testing Example
A school superintendent needs to document a rise in female students' math scores. Four standard testing practices all unintentionally activate the "women aren't good at math" stereotype. The research-based fixes:

1. **Room assignment:** Don't split by last-name letter. Women score worse on math in mixed-gender rooms (no such effect on verbal tests). Assign by gender — women-only rooms.
2. **Monitors:** Don't lottery-assign. Assign female math and science teachers to monitor the girls' rooms. Visible counter-stereotype role models immediately deflate stereotype impact.
3. **Pre-test minutes:** Don't let them "collect their thoughts about hard material" — that focuses them on the threat. Instead, have them pick a personally important value (friendship, helping others) and write briefly about why it matters. A single self-affirmation exercise, done twice in a semester, raised women's physics-exam scores by a full letter grade.
4. **Demographic form:** Don't ask students to record gender at the start of the math test. Replace with "year in school" (graduating senior — an *accomplishment* frame). Had been shown to eliminate the women's math-performance gap.

**Asian-American women experiment:** reminded of gender before a math test → worse performance. Reminded of ethnicity → *better* performance. Same women, same test. Attention activates whichever belief it touches.

## Mental Models
- **Where you sit is what you think.** Default environment = default associations = default output.
- **Units of experience in wait.** Everything you'd call "ambient sensation" or "latent ability" is addressable; attention is the address bus.
- **The "automagic" effect.** Not just automatic — it feels magical because the mechanism is invisible to the target.
- **Pre-suasion ethics applied to self is fine.** Pre-suading yourself toward truthful, goal-aligned output is the ethically cleanest version.

## Anti-patterns
- **Writing a popular book from a university office.** Wrong cues.
- **Cramming unknown material in the ten minutes before an exam.** Spikes anxiety, which drops performance.
- **Asking women to record gender before a math test.** Unintentionally activates the stereotype.
- **"Think about what might trip you up on this test."** Also wrong — focuses attention on threats you need to *not* be thinking about.
- **Dwelling on problems repeatedly.** Negative loop. Use Lyubomirsky's reframe activities to break out.
- **Assuming irrelevant surroundings are harmless.** The furniture of the room is doing its own persuading.

## Worked Example — The "Personalized Touch" Workaround
A manager noticed her team's work was better in glass-walled rooms with visible employees, worse in closed rooms. She couldn't pick the room (client's prerogative). Solution: download photos of client employees from their public site and internal publications, enlarge, mount on poster boards, lean around whatever conference room they get. Team gets the cue load it needs; clients love what feels like a personal touch. Pure pre-suasion on self, with a client-facing bonus.

## Key Takeaways
1. Environment carries cue load; cue load shapes output. Pick the room that matches the work.
2. Everything you want to feel more of (confidence, calm, creativity, gratitude) is already in you — attention is the switch.
3. Self-pre-suasion is ethically the cleanest zone to practice in. Start here.
4. For performance under stereotype threat: remove triggers, add counter-cues, use self-affirmation exercises.
5. Elderly people's happiness strategy is adoptable at any age — but it takes daily work.
6. The lever can import. If the physical geography is fixed, import the cues (posters, photos, sounds, scents).
