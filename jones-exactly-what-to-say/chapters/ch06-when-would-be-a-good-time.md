# Chapter 6: When Would Be a Good Time?

## Core Idea
"I don't have time" is the biggest reason ideas die. "When would be a good time to…?" presupposes that there *will* be a good time — removing "no" as an option and leaving only the when.

## The Magic Words
> **"When would be a good time to…?"**

## Why It Works
Presupposition. The sentence smuggles in an assumption — that a good time exists — and makes the only available answer a specific time. It converts an ask into a scheduling problem.

## When to Use
- Scheduling follow-ups, demos, next calls.
- Any moment where "I'm busy" is the likely stall.
- Early-stage: securing next contact without pitching.

## How
1. Attach it to a concrete action: "to take a proper look at this," "to get started," "to speak next."
2. When they reply with a time, **lock it** in the conversation — date, slot, channel. Don't leave it vague.
3. At that next contact, don't ask "what did you think of it?" (invites bad news). Ask **"What do you like about it?"** and let them list the positives.

## Worked Examples
- "When would be a good time for you to take a proper look at this?"
- "When would be a good time to get started?"
- "When would be a good time to speak next?"

## The Follow-up Move
> After they've agreed to next contact and you reach that point: swap "what did you think?" for **"What do you like about it?"**

This swap is small and critical. "What did you think?" licenses them to talk about objections. "What do you like?" licenses only positives — and listing positives moves the sale.

## Anti-patterns
- **Leaving the time vague** ("sometime next week"). The presupposition only works if you nail it down.
- **Asking "is there a good time?"** — reintroduces "no" as an option.
- **At follow-up, opening with "did you get a chance to look?"** — you've already lost the frame.

## Key Takeaways
1. Presupposition is a scheduling tool first, a persuasion tool second.
2. Lock the specific next contact — never let it float.
3. "What do you like about it?" belongs in your permanent script.
