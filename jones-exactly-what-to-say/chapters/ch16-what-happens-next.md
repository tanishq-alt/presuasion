# Chapter 16: What Happens Next

## Core Idea
After the pitch, don't ask what they want to do — *tell them* what happens next, and close on an easy question. Most sales die because the seller is too polite to narrate the obvious next step.

## The Magic Words
> **"What happens next is…"**
> **Close the narration with the easiest-possible question.**

## Why It Works
- **Fear of being pushy kills closes.** This phrase gives you a non-pushy script for what you've been avoiding.
- **Presupposition.** You're not asking if; you're describing when. The sale is already happening.
- **Easy closing question.** The easier the final question, the easier the yes. "What's your address?" closes better than "Would you like to proceed?"

## When to Use
- End of a successful presentation where they're nodding but not deciding.
- Any moment where you'd normally ask "So… what do you think?"
- When the conversation has stalled at completion.

## How
1. State **"What happens next is…"**
2. Narrate **two to three concrete steps** — details to capture, next meeting, support available.
3. Close with **a trivially easy question** — not "do you want to do this?" but "What's the best address for you?" or "What's your middle initial?"
4. The moment they answer, they're buying.

## Worked Example (from the book)
> "What happens next is that we are going to take a few moments, complete some of your personal details, and get things set up for you to receive everything in the quickest possible time.
>
> Then we need to schedule another meeting for us to get started, and at that point I am going to help you through all the steps to ensure that you realize your goals and are fully aware of all the support available to you.
>
> In terms of registering your details, what is the best address for you?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Ending with "So do you want to go ahead?"** You've undone all the work.
- **Too many steps in the narration.** Three is plenty; five feels like homework.
- **Hard closing question.** "What's your first-year budget allocation?" is wrong. Ask for an address, a middle initial, a phone number.

## Key Takeaways
1. The seller's job is to lead; not leading at the end is the most common failure mode.
2. Narrate the next steps as if the decision is already made.
3. The *easiest* question closes best — never the "most important" one.
