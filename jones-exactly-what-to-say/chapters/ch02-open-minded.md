# Chapter 2: How Open-Minded Are You?

## Core Idea
Ask "how open-minded are you about…?" and watch 90% of people say yes. The alternative — admitting you're closed-minded — is socially unacceptable, so the question presupposes agreement.

## The Magic Words
> **"How open-minded are you about…?"** / **"Would you be open-minded about…?"**

## Why It Works
Everyone thinks of themselves as open-minded. In a room of a thousand, ~900 would raise their hands. The question frames saying no to you as admitting the socially-ugly opposite. It shifts odds from 50-50 to roughly 90-10 in your favor.

## When to Use
- Introducing a brand-new idea to a stranger, prospect, friend, or team member.
- Any moment where "yes/no" feels too binary and you want a yes-by-default.
- As the second step after "I'm not sure if it's for you, but…" to narrow their response.

## How
1. Use "how" (sliding scale) when you want more engagement, "would" when you want a quick binary.
2. End the sentence with a specific action you want them to opt into, not a vague "this."
3. Let them answer before adding more.

## Worked Examples
- "How open-minded would you be about trying this as an alternative?"
- "Would you be open-minded about giving this a chance?"
- "How open-minded are you about increasing your monthly income?"
- "Would you be open-minded about seeing if we could work together?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Vague object** ("How open-minded are you about change?"). Too abstract — people say "it depends."
- **Tagging on "really" or "actually"**. Over-qualifies, telegraphs manipulation.
- **Firing it at someone who already said yes** — pointless.

## Key Takeaways
1. Presupposition moves odds faster than persuasion does.
2. Social identity (I am an open-minded person) is a stronger lever than logical argument.
3. Pair with a specific opt-in to convert the yes into a concrete next step.
