# Patterns — Exactly What to Say

Every pattern is a usable script. Say them in the order written, then **shut up**.

---

## 1. The Rejection-Free Opener
**When:** Introducing anything unsolicited.
**Script:** "I'm not sure if it's for you, but [the thing]."
**Trade-off:** Soft entry, moderate conversion. Pair with ch02 for stronger pull.

## 2. The Open-Minded Trap
**When:** Introducing a new idea; need a yes to proceed.
**Script:** "How open-minded are you about [specific opt-in]?"
**Trade-off:** Strong presumption-of-yes; can feel scripted if overused.

## 3. The Certainty Dissolver
**When:** They're arguing with confidence.
**Script:** "What do you know about [the domain their opinion rests on]?"
**Trade-off:** De-escalates; may extend conversation if they do have evidence.

## 4. Emotion-Then-Image Pair
**When:** Pre-close; decision stuck in logic.
**Script:** "How would you feel if [toward outcome]? And how would you feel if [away outcome]? … Just imagine [concrete scene]."
**Trade-off:** Strong motivator. Needs believable stakes.

## 5. The Scheduling Presupposition
**When:** Need to lock next contact.
**Script:** "When would be a good time to [specific action]?"
**Follow-up at that contact:** "What do you like about it?" (never "what did you think?")
**Trade-off:** High compliance; must lock the specific date/channel immediately.

## 6. The Face-Saving Chase
**When:** Chasing something they promised.
**Script:** "I'm guessing you haven't got around to [specific action]?"
**Trade-off:** Preserves relationship; needs both answer-branches prepared.

## 7. Simple Swap 1 — Invite Questions
**Install permanently.**
Instead of "Do you have any questions?" → **"What questions do you have for me?"**
**Trade-off:** Either you get specific blockers, or "none" = close signal.

## 8. Simple Swap 2 — Ask for Info
**Install permanently.**
Instead of "Can I have your [info]?" → **"What's the best [info] to use?"**
**Trade-off:** Removes permission-gate; near-100% compliance.

## 9. The Three-Options Stack Close
**When:** Stuck decision; they like you but won't commit.
**Script:**
> "As I see it, you have three options.
> First, you could [hard alternative — full labor spelled out].
> Second, you could [do nothing / stay stuck].
> Or third, you could [your offer — light and easy].
> Of those three options, what's going to be easier for you?"
**Trade-off:** Strong close; requires honest stacking — don't straw-man option 1.

## 10. The Two-Types Self-Sort
**When:** Identity-matters moment; near-decision.
**Script:** "There are two types of people in this world: [passive/outdated frame], and [agentic frame that maps to your offer]."
**Trade-off:** Binary forcing-function; don't pair with condescension.

## 11. The Pre-Loaded Agreement
**When:** Early conversation with strangers.
**Script:** "I bet you're a bit like me — [reasonable claim]."
**Trade-off:** Must call back later to cash the agreement, or wasted.

## 12. The If–Then Belief Lock
**When:** Promising benefit or consequence.
**Script:** "If [small action], then [specific outcome]."
**Trade-off:** Trades on trust — don't overstate "then."

## 13. The Anxiety-Defuser
**When:** Visible panic, nerves, hesitation.
**Script:** "Don't worry — [normalize] — [lead to next step]."
**Trade-off:** Requires calm tone; without the lead they stay worried.

## 14. The Most-People Nudge
**When:** Recommending action without ordering.
**Script:** "What most people do is [specific action matching your preferred next step]."
**Trade-off:** Social proof + covert advice; once per conversation.

## 15. The Positive Relabel
**When:** Self-doubt, resistance, pessimism.
**Script:** "The good news is [specific credible positive]."
**Excuse version:** "That's great — [reframe excuse as information]."
**Trade-off:** Watch delivery; sarcasm ruins it.

## 16. The Narration Close
**When:** End of pitch; they're nodding but not deciding.
**Script:**
> "What happens next is [step 1 — capture details]. Then [step 2 — next meeting / support]. In terms of [detail], what is [easy question]?"
**Trade-off:** Lead from the front; the easier the final question, the easier the yes.

## 17. The Universal Objection Reversal
**When:** Almost any common objection.
**Script:** "What makes you say that?"
**Trade-off:** Works everywhere; don't repeat on the same objection.

## 18. The Vantage-Shift Pullback
**When:** They're drifting toward no but haven't declared it.
**Script:** "Before you make your mind up, [new angle / review / another conversation]."
**Trade-off:** Only works before the no is stated.

## 19. The Conditional Close
**When:** Objection is a specific stated barrier.
**Script:** "If I can [solve their barrier], then will you [commit]?"
**Trade-off:** Secures yes before any concession; refuses reveal non-barrier objections.

## 20. The Quantity Upsize
**When:** Deliberating between two numbers.
**Script:** "Would [larger number] be enough for you?"
**Trade-off:** Direct closed question; backup: "What would be enough?"

## 21. The Columbo Downsell
**When:** Main ask failed; walking out.
**Script:** Pack up, step toward door, turn back: "Just one more thing — [smaller ask]."
**Options for smaller ask:** sample, trial, event invite, introduction, favor, scarcity question.
**Trade-off:** Only once per conversation; can't be repeated.

## 22. The Favor Pre-Commit → Referral Script
**When:** They just said "thank you."
**Full script:**
> You: "You couldn't do me a small favor, could you?"
> Them: (near-certain yes)
> You: "You wouldn't happen to know just one person — someone who, just like you, would benefit from [specific value they just thanked you for]?"
> [Shut up. Watch body language.]
> You (when they've thought of someone): "Don't worry — I'm not looking for their details right now, but who was it you were thinking of?"
> [Get the name. Ask when they'll see them next.]
> You: "You couldn't do me a further favor, could you? Next time you see [Name], could you share a little bit about how it was doing business with me and see if he's open-minded about taking a phone call from me to see if I can help him in the same way I helped you?"
> You: "Would it be okay if I gave you a call next week to find out how the chat with [Name] went?"
> [Next week:] You: "I'm guessing you didn't get around to speaking to [Name]?"

**Trade-off:** Slower than direct asks; produces warm, qualified leads.

## 23. The Permission-to-Ask-Rude-Question
**When:** You need honest information and the direct form would offend.
**Script:** "Just out of curiosity, [direct question]?"
**Then:** **Silence. 12 seconds minimum. Sit on your hands.**
**Trade-off:** The silence *is* the lever; if you fill it, the pattern collapses.

## Chaining Patterns

Use these sequences — not all 23 at once.

- **Cold → interest:** ch01 → ch02 → ch11
- **Argumentative prospect:** ch03 → ch15 → ch09
- **Stalled decision:** ch04 → ch05 → ch16
- **Objection cycle:** ch17 → ch18 → ch19
- **Follow-up:** ch06 → ch07 → ch16
- **Walking out empty-handed:** ch21 → ch22
- **"I need to think about it":** ch23 (→ silence → ch19 or ch16)
- **End of deal → more deals:** (close via ch16) → "thank you" trigger → ch22

**Rule:** never fire two openers back-to-back. Preface phrases burn fast if stacked.
