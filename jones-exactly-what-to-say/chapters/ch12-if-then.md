# Chapter 12: If… Then…

## Core Idea
Childhood pre-wired us to believe conditional statements. "If you do X, then Y will happen" lands as near-truth even for things we'd rationally question — because that's how parents and teachers programmed us.

## The Magic Words
> **"If [action], then [outcome]…"**

## Why It Works
- Conditional sentences were used on us thousands of times through childhood ("If you don't tidy your room, then you're grounded"). The pattern is baked in.
- When adults hear the same structure, the belief habit fires — the outcome feels guaranteed even if it isn't.
- "Then" implies logical necessity rather than probability.

## When to Use
- Promising an outcome or benefit.
- Painting consequence of inaction.
- Positioning a guarantee without using the word "guarantee."

## How
1. Make the "if" clause a *small, achievable* action — not a big commitment.
2. Make the "then" clause confident and specific.
3. Deliver evenly — don't push the "then" too hard or it reveals the structure.

## Worked Examples
- "If you decide to give this a try, then I promise you won't be disappointed."
- "If you put this in your stores, then I am certain your customers will like it."
- "If you give me a chance in the role, then I am confident you will thank me later."
- "If you're prepared to give this a try, then I am certain you'll see results as early as the first day you try it."

Childhood parallels (the mechanism):
- "If you don't eat all your dinner, then you're not going to get any dessert."
- "If you don't study hard at school, then you're not going to get into the college you're hoping for."
- "If you don't tidy your room, then you're going to be grounded for the weekend."

## Anti-patterns
- **Vague "then."** "If you try this, then good things will happen." Weak — specify.
- **Making promises you can't keep.** The whole structure trades on trust — don't burn it.
- **Overuse in a single conversation.** The pattern becomes visible if used more than once or twice.

## Key Takeaways
1. Formatting a claim as if/then makes it feel inevitable.
2. Keep "if" small and "then" specific.
3. The structure trades on childhood conditioning — honor the implicit promise.
