## Core Doctrine

**The job is to be a decision catalyst.** Most people stall not because they don't want to decide but because nobody helps them. The best words at the worst moment beat brilliant words at the right moment — so pre-load the phrases.

**Three operating truths behind every phrase:**
1. **Decisions are made first in imagination, then in reality.** If they can't picture it, they won't do it. Paint the picture.
2. **Loss > gain.** People will work harder to avoid a loss than to earn an equal gain. Frame both sides, lean on contrast.
3. **The subconscious hears the preface.** The first three to six words of a sentence set the frame the rest can't escape. "I'm not sure if it's for you, **but**…" reframes everything after as "look at this."

**Control = who's asking the question.** Every objection is a transfer of control from you to them. Reclaim it by answering with a question, not a counter-argument.

## The 23 Magic Phrases (by use-case)

### Opening & introducing an idea
- **"I'm not sure if it's for you, but…"** (ch01) — rejection-free opener; curiosity + "but" negates the no-pressure framing so they hear "look at this"
- **"How open-minded are you about…?"** (ch02) — forces a yes; the alternative is being closed-minded, which nobody admits to
- **"I bet you're a bit like me…"** (ch11) — pre-loads agreement with strangers; sets up later evidence you'll use to close

### Shifting their position
- **"What do you know about…?"** (ch03) — breaks the "I know best" wall by questioning the basis, not the conclusion
- **"How would you feel if…?"** (ch04) — time-travels them into the emotion; works both toward and away
- **"Just imagine…"** (ch05) — swaps your pitch for their own mental movie; harder to argue with
- **"If… then…"** (ch12) — childhood-conditioned belief structure; makes the outcome feel inevitable
- **"The good news is…"** (ch15) — labels a negative as a positive; the label sticks

### Driving the decision
- **"You have three options…"** (ch09) — collapse the choice; stack the first two as bad/worse, your pick last
- **"Two types of people…"** (ch10) — forces self-identification; make the choice you want the obvious one
- **"Most people…"** (ch14) — social proof + permission to advise without sounding bossy
- **"Would N be enough?"** (ch20) — nudges the larger quantity; direct yes/no, bigger number wins

### Follow-up & chasing
- **"When would be a good time to…?"** (ch06) — presupposes a yes; removes "I don't have time" as an option
- **"I'm guessing you haven't got around to…?"** (ch07) — gives them a face-saving excuse while pressing the point; they rise to the positive
- **"What happens next is…"** (ch16) — don't ask; narrate the next step and close with an easy question

### Objection handling & closing
- **"What makes you say that?"** (ch17) — universal objection reversal; makes them defend the objection with evidence
- **"Before you make your mind up…"** (ch18) — moves "no" back to "maybe" so negotiation can continue
- **"If I can…, will you…?"** (ch19) — isolates and neutralizes the stated barrier; conditional close
- **"Just one more thing…"** (ch21) — Columbo move; after the "no," try the downsell as they relax
- **"Just out of curiosity, what is it that…?"** (ch23) — asks rude/direct questions with permission; silence does the rest

### Asking for help / referrals
- **"Could you do me a small favor?"** (ch22) — pre-commitment before the ask; strongest after "thank you"

### Phrasing tweaks (small but structural)
- **"What questions do you have for me?"** (ch08, Simple Swap 1) — not "any questions?"; assumes they have some
- **"What's the best number to contact you at?"** (ch08, Simple Swap 2) — not "can I have your…?"; removes permission-gate
- **"Don't worry…"** (ch13) — two words; drops anxiety fast, lets you lead

## Universal rules

1. **After the Magic Words, shut up.** Silence is pressure on them, not you. Twelve seconds will feel like three weeks — sit on your hands.
2. **The preface is 80% of the work.** Never drop "I'm not sure if it's for you" or "Just out of curiosity" because it sounds awkward — the awkwardness *is* the softener.
3. **Hold eye contact and relaxed posture.** The words only work if you look like you mean them. Confidence is the carrier wave.
4. **Chain, don't stack.** Pair phrases in sequence (e.g. "What makes you say that?" → "If I can, will you?" → "What happens next is…"). Never fire two openers back-to-back.
5. **Honest reasons only.** The phrases are rhetorical levers, not lies. Jones's test: if the result wouldn't hold up with an honest third party watching, don't use them.

## Scope & Limits

Short-form influence tactics for 1:1 conversations and small-group sales. Does not cover written copy at scale, pricing strategy, or psychology beyond what Jones uses as grounding (loss aversion, imagination, social proof). The phrases are tools — Jones's own test is whether they'd hold up in front of an honest observer. Combine with a product you'd actually recommend.
