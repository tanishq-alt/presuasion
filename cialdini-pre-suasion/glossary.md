# Glossary — Pre-Suasion

**Agenda Setting** — media rarely tells people what to think; it tells them what to think about. (Ch3)

**Anchoring (opener)** — a number or stimulus delivered first that reframes everything following. "Not going to charge you a million dollars." (Ch1)

**Attentional Blink** — ~half-second dead zone during a shift of focus; newly highlighted information can't register consciously. (Ch2)

**Attractors** — three features that automatically command attention: sexual, threatening, different. (Ch5)

**Away motivation** — the drive to avoid loss. Stronger than toward motivation (loss aversion). (Ch5; ch10 scarcity)

**Camera-angle effect** — whichever face is more visible in a two-person recording is judged more causal. Reverses when the angle is flipped. (Ch4)

**Channeled attention** — directing a target's attention to a selected concept via opener. Mechanism of pre-suasion. (Ch2–6)

**Commitment** — active, effortful, voluntary behavioral step that locks a preference into self-perception and makes it durable. (Ch10, ch14)

**Consider-the-opposite** — debiasing procedure forcing "what could go wrong?" and "what happens if it does?" Produces 5–7% ROI advantage for firms using sound judgment-debiasing. (Ch3)

**Consistency principle** — people align with their own prior public commitments. Six of the seven universal principles. (Ch10)

**Co-creation** — joint creation bonds the co-creators; the IKEA effect extended to people. Managers who helped make an ad rated both themselves AND the employee as more responsible. (Ch12)

**Credible authority** — expertise + trustworthiness. Trustworthiness is the usually-missing half; mention a weakness first to install it. (Ch10)

**Customization** — the strongest of the three reciprocity multipliers. A gift fitted to the recipient's specific need. (Ch10)

**Difference (attractor)** — orienting response to change in environment; novelty; decoy-by-contrast. (Ch5)

**Dread risk** — risky action taken to avoid a focal threat that is actually safer than the alternative. Post-9/11 driving. (Ch5)

**Easy / Fluency** — processing fluency is read as truth/value. Rhyme boosts perceived truth. Hard-to-pronounce names underperform. (Ch7)

**Embedded reporters program** — Iraq 2003. Shifted journalist task → shifted public attention → masked the war's weakest leg (WMD). (Ch3)

**Ethical use (triple-tumor structure)** — unethical cultures produce poor performance, high turnover, and employee fraud, even without external detection. (Ch13)

**Fictive family** — collectives using familial labels (brothers, sisterhood, forefathers, motherland) to produce real-kin levels of loyalty and sacrifice. (Ch11)

**Fire when readied** — mental elements don't just fire when ready; they fire when readied. Linked concepts become accessible, unlinked concepts suppressed. (Ch9)

**Fit-in vs. stand-out** — threat primes "join the crowd"; sex primes "stand out." Mismatch kills ad effectiveness. (Ch5)

**Focal is causal** — focused attention transfers perceived causality. (Ch4)

**Focusing Illusion (Kahneman)** — "Nothing in life is as important as you think it is while you are thinking about it." (Ch3)

**Helping Norm / Norm of Social Responsibility** — people help in proportion to perceived need. Visible need triggers obligation. (Ch4)

**Home (place-based unity)** — those with whom you grew up are treated as kin. Children exposed to diverse strangers in the home develop broader we-ness. (Ch11)

**Hyperlink / prefetch metaphor** — major goals are pre-fetched; one cue (one click) launches them. (Ch9)

**If/when-then plans** — "If/when [specific cue], then [specific action]." Self-installed cue → action link. (Ch9, ch14)

**Implicit egoism** — overvaluation of entities linked to the self (names, initials, birthdays). (Ch7)

**IKEA Effect** — people rate their own built items as comparable in value to expert-built items. Extends to co-creators. (Ch12)

**In-group vs out-group** — unity principle favors in-group members automatically. Synchrony with an out-group member reduces this bias. (Ch11, ch12)

**Kinship** — direct genetic unity. Brain self-reward centers fire when helping family. 97% parent response to Cialdini's questionnaire for a single test point. (Ch11)

**Language as influence (not conveyance)** — Semin's psycholinguistics: language's main job is to route attention. (Ch7)

**Liking principle** — people say yes to those they like. Actually: *to those who they feel like them.* Install via similarities + genuine compliments. (Ch10)

**Localism** — favoritism toward those physically proximate. Concentration-camp guard + home-town prisoner. Trocmé's Le Chambon rescues through relatives/neighbors. (Ch11)

**Loss aversion** — losses outweigh equivalent gains; scarcity's engine. (Ch10)

**Magnetizers** — three features that hold attention: self-relevant, unfinished, mysterious. (Ch6)

**Meaningful + Unexpected + Customized** — the three multipliers of a gift's reciprocity return. (Ch10)

**Metaphor (meta-door)** — transfers both meaning and implied solution. "Crime as beast" vs "crime as virus" changes preferred solution by 22%. (Ch7)

**Moment maker vs moment monitor** — practitioner who creates privileged moments vs. one who recognizes them in the wild. (Ch1)

**Moral stress** — conflict between an employee's values and the perceived organizational ethics. Produces fatigue and burnout; degrades performance more than typical workplace stressors. (Ch13)

**Mysteries (teaching tactic)** — require explanation; recruit integration of all facts. Six-step structure: pose → deepen → consider alternatives → clue → resolve → draw implication. (Ch6)

**Next-in-line effect** — self-focus before or after your turn in a group prevents you from processing adjacent speakers. Implication: don't sit next to the decision-maker. Sit across. (Ch6)

**Openers** — frames, anchors, primes, mindsets, first impressions. Cialdini's umbrella term for the pre-suasive device. (Ch1)

**Orienting Response (O-wave)** — ancestral attention hijack to environmental change. Pavlov's "investigatory reflex." (Ch5)

**Place (persuasive geography)** — physical location's cue load shapes thought. Both external (where you work) and internal (what's activated inside you). (Ch8)

**Positive Test Strategy** — when deciding if a possibility is true, people hunt for confirmations and not disconfirmations. Fuel for single-chute questions. (Ch2)

**Post-suasion** — making pre-suasive shifts durable via commitment + daily cues. (Ch14)

**Pre-fetched goals** — major goals exist in standby; one reminder cue launches them. (Ch9)

**Pre-suasion** — arranging recipients to be receptive to a message before they encounter it. The book's central concept. (Ch1)

**Privileged Moment** — time-limited window after a pre-suasive opener when the listener is unusually vulnerable to aligned requests. (Ch1, ch2)

**Reciprocation principle** — people say yes to those they owe. Give first, meaningful + unexpected + customized. (Ch10)

**Reciprocal self-disclosure (Arons' 36 Questions)** — escalating turn-taking personal disclosures build intense closeness in 45 minutes. (Ch12)

**Reminders** — simple attention redirects to already-held knowledge; usually enough to neutralize bias. "To re-mind" = put back in mind. (Ch9)

**Rhyme-as-reason** — rhyming aphorisms judged more true. ("Caution and measure win you treasure.") (Ch7)

**Romance of Leadership** — leaders are assigned causal weight beyond their actual influence; workforce quality, systems, market conditions drive more of results. (Ch4)

**Satisficing (Simon)** — satisfy + suffice; decision makers pick the first good-enough option rather than optimizing. (Ch3)

**Scarcity principle** — we want more of what we can have less of; loss framing > gain framing; purchase limits more than double sales. (Ch10)

**Self-affirmation** — writing about a personal value shifts attention from stereotype threat to interpersonal strength; women's physics-grade gain of a full letter. (Ch8)

**Self-other overlap** — unity feature: mental representations of self and close others share brain circuitry. We is the shared me. (Ch11)

**Self-relevant (magnetizer)** — attention superglue. "You" instead of "they" in ad copy. (Ch6)

**Sexual (attractor)** — attention-pull limited to sexually-adjacent product categories; Valentine Street study. (Ch5)

**Shared identity** — the real lever of unity: "that person is of us" (vs liking's "like us"). (Ch11)

**Single-chute question** — one-sided framing that routes memory search down one direction, biasing self-concept the respondent reports. ("Are you adventurous?") (Ch2)

**Six Main Roads** — the six principles from *Influence*: reciprocation, liking, social proof, authority, scarcity, consistency. (Ch10)

**Social Proof principle** — people model similar others. Two levers: validity (what's right) and feasibility (what's doable). (Ch10)

**Stereotype threat** — reminding of a negative in-group stereotype degrades performance. Reminding of a positive one improves it. (Ch8)

**Sticky commitment** — active + effortful + voluntary; the triad that locks pre-suasive shifts into identity. (Ch14)

**Synchrony** — matched motor/sensory/vocal activity produces felt identity merger. Marching, music, finger-tapping, face-brushing. (Ch12)

**System 1 vs System 2** (Kahneman) — fast/associative/emotional vs slow/deliberative/analytical. Match the system of your appeal to the audience's current system. Music is System 1. (Ch12)

**Threatening (attractor)** — commands attention; works for behavior change only when paired with a specific actionable step; otherwise triggers denial. (Ch5)

**Three Options (close)** — stacked-choice structure where your pick is the path of least resistance. See Jones skill ch09. (Cross-reference)

**Toward motivation** — drive to acquire a gain. Weaker than away motivation. Use in balance. (Ch5)

**Triple-Tumor Structure** — poor performance, high turnover, employee fraud. The three internal consequences of an unethical organizational culture. (Ch13)

**Trustworthiness** — the trait most wanted in an interaction partner. Install fast by mentioning a weakness before the strength (Elizabeth I: "body of a weak and feeble woman; *but* heart of a king"). (Ch10, ch11)

**Two-sided questions** — balanced framing ("satisfied or dissatisfied"). The corrective for single-chute bias. (Ch2)

**Unexpected (reciprocity multiplier)** — the waitress returning with a second chocolate after walking away: +21.3% tip. (Ch10)

**Unfinished tasks (Zeigarnik effect)** — more memorable than completed ones; pulls attention back until closure. Lever behind cliffhangers and writing cliffhanger-stopping. (Ch6)

**Unity principle (seventh)** — shared identity: "of us, not like us." Kinship + place + synchrony + co-creation + advice-asking. (Ch11, ch12)

**Validity (social proof)** — if many similar others do X, X seems morally and practically correct. (Ch10)

**"We is the shared me"** — Cialdini's one-line summary of unity. (Ch11)

**What's focal is causal** — paired with "what's salient is important"; together they define the downstream distortion of channeled attention. (Ch3, ch4)

**Word choice** — associations attach to words; "bullet points" vs "information points" triggers different frames. (Ch7)

**Zeigarnik effect** — see Unfinished tasks. (Ch6)
