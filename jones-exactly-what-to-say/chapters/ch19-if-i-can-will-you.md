# Chapter 19: If I Can…, Will You…?

## Core Idea
When the objection names a specific condition ("if only the price/schedule/terms were different…"), isolate the condition with a conditional close: "If I can [solve the condition], will you [commit]?" You haven't agreed to anything yet, but you've locked in their yes.

## The Magic Words
> **"If I can…, then will you…?"**

## Why It Works
- **Isolates the objection.** Forces them to decide whether that condition is the *actual* barrier or just an excuse.
- **Pre-commits them to the close.** If you solve it, they've already said yes.
- **Reveals real objections.** If they say "yes, if you can," great. If they say "well, there's also…" you've surfaced the real issue.
- **You haven't committed yet.** You're still in control of whether to meet the condition.

## When to Use
- Negotiation objections: price, terms, timing, logistics.
- Personal asks where someone blames an external condition ("I can't because I don't have transportation").
- Any time the obstacle sounds solvable.

## How
1. Listen for the specific condition they named.
2. Respond with: "If I can [specific solution to their condition], will you [specific commitment]?"
3. Wait for their answer.
4. Only *then* decide if you'll actually meet the condition.

## Worked Examples
- Friend: "I can't come Friday because my car is in the shop and buses don't run that late."
  → You: "If I can pick you up and drop you off at home, then will you be ready for seven pm?"
- Prospect: "I'd like a price that matches competitor X."
  → You: "If I can match that price for you, then would you be happy to place the order with me today?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Committing to the condition before getting their commitment.** You've lost the leverage.
- **Vague "if I can help" phrasings.** Specificity is the entire mechanism.
- **Promising conditions you can't meet.** Burns trust and future conversations.
- **Using it when the objection isn't a condition** ("I don't know, I just don't feel ready"). Use ch17 or ch23 instead.

## Key Takeaways
1. The conditional close secures a yes before you've given anything up.
2. If they refuse the conditional, they weren't really objecting to that condition.
3. Always let them commit first; then decide.
