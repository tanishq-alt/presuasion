# Chapter 11: Unity 1 — Being Together

## Core Idea
Beyond the six principles of *Influence*, there's a seventh — **Unity**. Unity isn't "that person is *like* us"; it's "that person is *of* us." Same identity, not merely similar features. Where the liking principle pulls because of surface overlap, unity pulls because of merged selves. The concept of self and the concept of close others share brain circuitry. **"We is the shared me."** Two chapters cover unity: being together (shared identity by kinship or place) and acting together (synchrony, collaboration — ch12).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Unity (seventh principle).** Shared identities — race, ethnicity, nationality, family, politics, religion — in which one member's conduct affects another member's self-esteem.
- **Of us vs. like us.** The critical distinction. Liking ≠ Unity.
- **Inclusive fitness** (evolutionary biology) — "self-interest" extends to copies of your genes in related others. Supports the kinship pull.
- **Fictive family** — imagined kinship produced by familial labels (brothers, sisterhood, forefathers, motherland, heritage). Produces real-kin levels of self-sacrifice.
- **Localism** — favoritism toward those physically proximate; neighborhood, town, region, nation.

## Being Together — Two Pathways

### 1. Kinship

- Family members — direct genetic relation — get the strongest unity pull. Brain-imaging shows **self-reward centers fire when aiding family**, as if helping self.
- **The one-point study (Cialdini's own):** needed parents of 163 students to fill out a long questionnaire. Adult response rates in such surveys usually <20%. He offered students one extra point on the next test. Result: 163/163 sent it, 159 (97%) returned completed within a week. The kinship card beat every incentive mechanism he'd tried in his career. (He speculates a grandparent would have gotten 162/163 back — "the missing one from a hospitalization after sprinting to the post office.")
- **Fictive family labels:** reminding Spaniards their nation is like a family led "fused" citizens to become dramatically more willing to fight and die for Spain.
- **Warren Buffett's 2015 Berkshire fiftieth-anniversary letter.** With concerns rising that Buffett and Munger were aging and the stock might fall, Buffett addressed the "Next 50 Years" with pre-suasion. He first established trustworthiness by admitting a mistake (50-year predictions would have been off). Then he invoked family explicitly: *"I will tell you what I would say to my family today if they asked me about Berkshire's future."* Cialdini: "He had me at family." Not trickery — a genuinely trustworthy communicator wielding the family frame honestly.
- **Facial similarity as a kin cue.** We use faces to estimate genetic relatedness. Digitally morphed candidate photos that look more like the viewer increase their trust and voting likelihood. Can be tricked.

### 2. Place — Home, Locality, Region

**Home** (narrowest):
- Humans and animals treat those raised in their home as family.
- Children who see parents welcome diverse strangers into the home develop broader we-ness and help strangers as adults.
- **Chiune Sugihara** — Japanese consul in Lithuania (1940) who wrote travel visas for ~thousands of Jews in defiance of Tokyo's three successive refusals. Lost his career. One likely root: as a child in Korea, his parents ran an inn and he saw them feed, shelter, and bathe guests regardless of ability to pay. Sugihara: nationality and religion didn't matter — the Jews were members with him in the human family.

**Locality:**
- Cohen's concentration-camp story: a veteran guard performing routine ten-count executions suddenly skipped a victim who happened to be from the guard's home town, executing the eleventh instead. "Mutuality of place" was sufficient to trigger mercy inside mass murder.
- **The pattern of Holocaust rescuers:** most didn't spontaneously seek out victims. The direct request came from a *relative or neighbor* of the rescuer, petitioning on behalf of a hunted individual. Rescuers said yes to their own kin and neighbors, not to strangers. **Pastor André Trocmé** of Le Chambon-sur-Lignon — saved thousands — operated by asking relatives and neighbors first, then pressing them to ask their own relatives and neighbors.

**Region:**
- Sports-team championships produce personal pride in nearby residents.
- Citizens more likely to participate in surveys from home-state universities.
- Readers of Afghanistan casualty stories become more anti-war when the fallen soldier is from their own state.
- Civil War infantrymen deserted less when their comrades came from the same region.
- **Rabbi Shimon Kalisch's line** (Japan, 1941). The Gestapo's "Butcher of Warsaw" pressed Tokyo for a Final Solution extension to Japan's Jews. Japan's High Command summoned two rabbis and asked: *Why do our allies the Nazis hate you so? And why should we take your side?* The scholarly Rabbi Shatzkes had no ready answer. Rabbi Kalisch, the "social psychologist of sorts," said calmly: **"Because we are Asian, like you."** The observation shifted the Japanese officers' in-group identity from wartime alliance to regional/genetic kinship. The Nazis' own master-race doctrine made the Jews the Asians' allies, not the Nazis. The senior officer promised: *"Go back to your people. Tell them we will provide for their safety and peace."* Japan resisted Nazi pressure for the rest of the war.

## Mental Models
- **"We is the shared me."** Unity isn't similarity — it's identity merger. Different circuitry, different lever.
- **Kin cues can be real or symbolic.** Genetic relation, physical proximity, familial labels, morphed faces — all plug into the same slot.
- **The weakness → family frame → strength sequence** (Buffett's letter). Order matters: trustworthiness first via admitted weakness, then unity frame, then strengths that now land as advice from family.
- **Favor the one who is of us.** Not "I would help this stranger" — but "I would help my relative's neighbor."
- **The medium, the multitude, the messenger, the merger.** Four persuasive dimensions beyond message merit: medium (McLuhan), multitude (social proof), messenger (authority), merger (unity).

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating liking and unity as the same lever.** Liking is "like us"; unity is "of us." Salesman smile and mimicry create liking, not unity.
- **Pitching to a stranger in isolation.** Route through someone already in-group to them.
- **Appealing to generic "humanity."** Can work (Sugihara, Trocmé) but much weaker than specific identity frames.
- **Over-widening the family label.** Cheap use of "we/family/brotherhood" burns the lever.
- **Burying the honesty.** Buffett opens annual reports with mistakes and problems. Without that, the family-frame pivot wouldn't land.

## Worked Example — Buffett's Family Frame
In the 2015 Berkshire letter, after admitting that his own 50-year-ago predictions would have been off, Buffett wrote: *"With that warning, I will tell you what I would say to my family today if they asked me about Berkshire's future."* He then enumerated the business model, financial bulwark, and vetted next CEO. Cialdini (a Berkshire shareholder) had already been nervous about Buffett and Munger's age. After the letter, he never seriously considered selling. The pre-suasive family frame transformed the same business arguments into advice from kin.

## Key Takeaways
1. Unity is a distinct principle; it operates via merged identity, not resemblance.
2. Kinship levers: real family, fictive family (labels, imagery), facial similarity.
3. Place levers: home (and who was welcomed in it), locality (town, neighborhood), region.
4. Favors routed through an already-in-group intermediary beat direct appeals to strangers.
5. In crisis communication (Buffett): weakness admission → kin frame → arguments → audience now trusts and treats arguments as advice from family.
6. Rabbi Kalisch's single sentence is the compressed template: find the broader identity that puts you in-group with the decision-maker and *excludes* the counter-party.
