# Chapter 18: Before You Make Your Mind Up

## Core Idea
Moving someone from "no" to "yes" is nearly impossible. Moving them from "no" to "maybe" is easy. "Before you make your mind up…" buys you a different vantage point before the no hardens.

## The Magic Words
> **"Before you make your mind up…"**

## Why It Works
- **Preserves a path back.** The listener hasn't yet declared the no publicly — which means you haven't hit the wall of ego defense yet.
- **Invites a vantage shift.** By definition, "before you make your mind up" signals there's one more angle to consider.
- **Reasonable framing.** Hard to refuse a request for one more look before deciding.

## When to Use
- Prospect is leaning toward no but hasn't said it firmly.
- After a tough objection has been aired (often follows ch17 "what makes you say that?").
- To keep the negotiation alive when it feels over.

## How
1. Interrupt the drift toward no immediately — don't let the no be stated.
2. Preface with "Before you make your mind up…"
3. Add either: a specific new angle, a review of the facts, or one more conversation.
4. Make the reason for the pause feel reasonable to *them*, not convenient for *you*.

## Worked Examples
- "Look, before you make your mind up, let's make sure we've looked at all the facts."
- "Before you make your mind up, why don't we just run through the details one more time so you can know what it is that you are saying no to?"
- "Before you make your mind up, wouldn't it make sense to speak to a few more people about the difference this could make for you and your family?"

## Anti-patterns
- **After a firm, stated no.** The move's power is in *before*. After, use "just out of curiosity" (ch23).
- **Adding pressure.** The phrase works because it feels non-pressuring; don't ruin it with urgency.
- **Giving them no new material.** The pause has to be *for* something — a fact, a reframe, a conversation.

## Key Takeaways
1. The no is sticky once declared; prevent the declaration.
2. A vantage shift is enough — you don't need to argue them back.
3. "Maybe" is the only station between "no" and "yes." Buy the ticket.
