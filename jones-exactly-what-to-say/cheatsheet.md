# Cheatsheet — Exactly What to Say

Decision rules, not definitions. Everything here should help you *pick a phrase*.

---

## Pick-A-Phrase by Scenario

| Situation | Say this | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Opening cold | "I'm not sure if it's for you, but…" | Rejection-free; "but" negates |
| Need buy-in to hear more | "How open-minded are you about…?" | Social identity forces yes |
| They're arguing with certainty | "What do you know about…?" | Question the basis, not the conclusion |
| Pre-close (emotion missing) | "How would you feel if [toward]? And if [away]?" | Loss > gain; contrast moves |
| Decision stuck | "Just imagine…" | Decision made in mind first |
| Need to schedule | "When would be a good time to…?" | Presupposes yes |
| Follow-up next contact | "What do you like about it?" | Don't ask "what did you think?" — invites bad news |
| Chasing someone silent | "I'm guessing you haven't got around to…?" | Face-saving, binary branches |
| End of pitch | "What questions do you have for me?" | Presumes they have some; "none" = close signal |
| Asking for info | "What's the best [X] to use?" | Not "can I have…?" |
| Choosing to commit | "You have three options…" | Stack your pick as path of least resistance |
| Identity moment | "There are two types of people…" | Forced self-sort |
| Stranger, cold agreement | "I bet you're a bit like me…" | Pre-loaded nod |
| Promising a benefit | "If…, then…" | Childhood conditional lock |
| They're anxious | "Don't worry — [normalize] — [lead]" | Calm + direct |
| Recommending without pushing | "Most people…" | Social proof + covert advice |
| Negative loop | "The good news is…" / "That's great" | Labels stick |
| At the close | "What happens next is… [easy question]" | Narrate, don't ask |
| Any common objection | "What makes you say that?" | Universal reversal |
| Leaning no | "Before you make your mind up…" | Buy a vantage shift |
| Stated barrier | "If I can…, will you…?" | Isolate + conditional close |
| Quantity deliberation | "Would [N] be enough?" | Bigger number, closed question |
| Main ask failed | (Walk out, turn back) "Just one more thing…" | Guard-down downsell |
| Just heard "thank you" | "Could you do me a small favor?" | Pre-commit before the ask |
| "I need to think about it" | "Just out of curiosity, what is it specifically you need to think about?" | Then: silence, 12 seconds |

## Objection → Response Table

| Objection | Primary response | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| "I need to think about it" | "Just out of curiosity, what is it specifically you need to think about?" | Silence 12s |
| "I don't have the time" | "What makes you say that?" | Then "If I can…, will you…?" |
| "I don't have the money" | "What makes you say that?" | Then "If I can…, will you…?" |
| "I need to speak to [person]" | "What makes you say that?" | Then isolate: "If they said yes, would you?" |
| "I want to shop around" | "Before you make your mind up, let's make sure we've looked at all the facts" | Re-present or ch19 |
| "I'm not sure I can do it" | "The good news is…" | Normalize + evidence |
| "I need to think about it" (repeat) | "Just one more thing…" | Downsell to a smaller commitment |

## Permanent Installs (not "phrases to try" — reflexes to build)

1. Catch yourself saying **"Do you have any questions?"** → swap to **"What questions do you have for me?"**
2. Catch yourself saying **"Can I have your…?"** → swap to **"What's the best … to use?"**
3. On the follow-up call after a demo, never ask **"What did you think?"** → always **"What do you like about it?"**
4. When they say **"Thank you,"** your reflex: **"Could you do me a small favor?"**
5. When they're stuck between two numbers, default to **"Would [bigger] be enough?"**

---

## Universal Rules (no phrase works without these)

1. **Silence after the ask.** Twelve seconds minimum. Sit on your hands.
2. **Deliver matter-of-factly.** Confidence is the carrier wave. If you sound uncertain, the phrase sounds manipulative.
3. **Never stack two openers.** ch01 + ch02 fine. ch01 + ch01 obvious.
4. **Lock the next contact** when scheduling. Date + channel.
5. **Chain, don't dump.** Pick 2–3 phrases per conversation, not all 23.

## The Integrity Test

Jones's own test, implicit in the book:
**Would the result of your using this phrase hold up if an honest third party watched the whole conversation?**

If yes → use freely.
If no → you're manipulating, not influencing. Don't.

## Model-of-the-Buyer (one-page)

1. **Decisions are made first in imagination.** If they can't see it, they won't do it.
2. **Emotion first, logic second.** Something feels right before it makes sense.
3. **Loss > gain.** Avoiding pain moves them harder than gaining pleasure.
4. **Identity is sticky.** Open-minded people stay open-minded; "most people" people follow "most people."
5. **Agreement is cheap and compounds.** Build nods early; cash them at close.
6. **Silence is work.** The question is the setup; the silence does the work.
7. **People are mostly people of their word.** Calling them out gently makes them rise, not retreat.
