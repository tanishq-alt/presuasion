# Glossary — Exactly What to Say

**Away motivation** — the drive to avoid a potential loss or pain. Stronger than toward motivation. (ch04)

**Before you make your mind up…** — phrase that moves someone from leaning "no" back to "maybe" by inviting a vantage shift. (ch18)

**"But" negation** — the three-letter pivot that makes listeners remember only what follows it. Core to "I'm not sure if it's for you, but…" and "Don't worry… but…" (ch01, ch13)

**Closed question** — a yes/no form (e.g. "Would 8 be enough?"); contrasted with open questions. The "Enough" close uses this form. (ch20)

**Columbo moment** — turning back at the door for "just one more thing" after the guard is down. (ch21)

**Conditional close** — "If I can… will you…?" — secures yes before giving anything up. (ch19)

**Covert advice** — telling someone what to do without appearing to ("Most people would…"). (ch14)

**Decision catalyst** — Jones's name for the real job of sales: helping people decide. Also "professional mind-maker-upper." (overview, ch09, ch23)

**Decision made twice** — every decision is made first in imagination, then in reality. If they can't see it, they won't do it. (ch05)

**Don't worry** — anxiety-defuser; two words that reset tone and let you lead. (ch13)

**Downsell** — asking for a smaller commitment after the main ask has failed. (ch21)

**Easy closing question** — final narration question that is trivially easy to answer (address, middle initial) — the easier the question, the easier the yes. (ch16)

**Enough** — anchor word; "Would N be enough?" directs toward the larger quantity. (ch20)

**Favor hook** — "Could you do me a small favor?" — pre-commits agreement before the ask is named. (ch22)

**Good news is…** — label applied to any negative to force a positive reframe. (ch15)

**How open-minded are you?** — presupposed-yes opener leveraging social identity. (ch02)

**How would you feel if…?** — time-travels the listener into a future emotion, toward or away. (ch04)

**I bet you're a bit like me…** — pre-loads strangers' agreement on a reasonable premise to bank for later. (ch11)

**I'm guessing you haven't got around to…?** — face-saving chase for unfulfilled commitments. (ch07)

**I'm not sure if it's for you, but…** — rejection-free opener. (ch01)

**If… then…** — conditional belief frame rooted in childhood conditioning. (ch12)

**If I can…, will you…?** — conditional close that isolates a stated barrier. (ch19)

**Just imagine…** — opens the listener's image viewer to paint their own scene. (ch05)

**Just one more thing…** — Columbo move; downsell at the moment of departure. (ch21)

**Just out of curiosity…** — preface granting permission to ask direct/rude questions. (ch23)

**Labeling** — attaching a name to a situation that the listener must accept or argue. "The good news is…" / "That's great…" use this. (ch15)

**Loss aversion** — the real-world finding that people work harder to avoid a loss than to achieve an equivalent gain. Core to "how would you feel if…" and the three-options stack. (ch04, ch09)

**Magic Words** — Jones's term for the set of preface-phrases in the book — small, structural swaps in the opening of a sentence that redirect the conversation. (all)

**Motivation** — "motive + action" = a reason to move. Big enough reasons move anyone. (ch04)

**Most people…** — social proof + covert advice. Credited by Jones with more negotiating wins than any other phrase. (ch14)

**Objection = shift of control** — every objection transfers control to the other person. Reclaim with a question. (ch17)

**Open question** — a question answered with information rather than yes/no. Used in Simple Swap 1: "What questions do you have for me?" rather than "Do you have any?" (ch08)

**Path of least resistance** — the choice framed as the easiest; the three-options stack makes your pick the path of least resistance. (ch09)

**Preface** — first few words of a sentence that set the frame for everything after. The entire book is about prefaces. (all)

**Presupposition** — grammatical smuggling of an assumption into a question: "When would be a good time?" presupposes a good time exists. (ch06, ch08, ch16, ch20)

**Professional mind-maker-upper** — Jones's informal name for the seller's job. (ch10, ch23)

**Rhythm of three** — three options feel complete and digestible; more feels confusing, fewer feels binary. (ch09)

**Rejection-free opener** — opener engineered so no response is a rejection. "I'm not sure if it's for you, but…" is the exemplar. (ch01)

**Simple Swap** — small word change with a large control effect. Two canonical swaps: "What questions do you have for me?" and "What's the best number to contact you at?" (ch08)

**Silence (after the ask)** — the operator behind "just out of curiosity," "most people," the favor script, and the three-options close. Twelve seconds will feel like three weeks. (ch17, ch22, ch23)

**Social proof** — people take confidence from the fact that people like them have made the decision before them. Lever behind "most people." (ch14)

**That's great** — companion to "the good news is…" — circuit-breaker for excuse spirals. (ch15)

**Three options** — close that stacks the choices so your pick is the path of least resistance, closed with "what's easier for you?" (ch09)

**Toward motivation** — drive to acquire a potential gain. Weaker than away motivation, used in balance with it. (ch04)

**Two types of people** — binary self-identification forcing a self-sort. (ch10)

**Vantage shift** — reason given for pausing a decision: new facts, another conversation, another look. Core to ch18. (ch18)

**What do you know about…?** — question that dissolves certainty by challenging its basis rather than its conclusion. (ch03)

**What happens next is…** — narration of the close rather than permission-asking at the close. (ch16)

**What makes you say that?** — universal objection-reversal question. (ch17)

**When would be a good time to…?** — presupposed-yes scheduling phrase. (ch06)

**Would N be enough?** — direct closed question on quantity with the larger option. (ch20)

**You have three options…** — see "three options." (ch09)
