# Chapter 14: Most People

## Core Idea
"Most people" does two jobs at once: it's social proof ("safe — others like me chose this") and it's polite advice ("here's what to do, without me telling you what to do"). Jones credits it with more negotiating wins than any other phrase.

## The Magic Words
> **"Most people…"** / **"What most people do is…"**

## Why It Works
- **Social proof:** confidence rises when the listener believes others like them have chosen before them and it worked out. (The kids-jumping-off-the-rock metaphor: one person goes, then everyone goes.)
- **Covert advice:** you want to say "what I think you should do is…" but that's bossy. "Most people" lets you tell them what to do without appearing to tell them.
- **Self-identification:** the listener's subconscious says "I'm most people — so that's what I should do too."

## When to Use
- Prospects stuck in indecision or procrastination.
- When you want to recommend a specific action without sounding pushy.
- Immediately before asking for the decision.

## How
1. State what "most people" do in the specific scenario.
2. Describe it in a way that matches the next step you want.
3. Add a soft framing of risk-reduction ("knowing there's almost no risk," "see how it works for them").
4. Pause — they'll start justifying the action to themselves.

## Worked Examples
- "What most people do is complete the forms with me here today. You then receive your welcome pack and we get you booked in for a launch."
- "What most people do is place a small order to get started, commit to a few of the best products, see how they work out in their daily routines, and then decide what they want to do next."
- "Most people in your circumstances would grab this opportunity with both hands, knowing that there is almost no risk."

## Anti-patterns
- **"Most people would be stupid to miss this."** Insulting and loses the covert-advice value.
- **False claims.** If "most people" didn't actually do that, you've burned trust.
- **Overuse.** Once per conversation; twice makes it visible.

## Key Takeaways
1. Social proof + permission-to-advise is why this phrase outperforms most others.
2. People follow others *like them* — be specific about the comparable group.
3. It's a reco disguised as an observation. Keep the disguise.
