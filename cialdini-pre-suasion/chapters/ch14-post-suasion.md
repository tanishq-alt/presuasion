# Chapter 14: Post-Suasion — Aftereffects

## Core Idea
Pre-suasion channels attention temporarily. To make the resulting shift *durable*, pre-suaders need two things: (1) a **commitment** taken in an active, effortful, voluntary way that locks the new preference into self-perception, and (2) a **cue-rich environment** that keeps the preference top-of-mind in daily recurrence. "Who we are with respect to any choice is where we are, attentionally, in the moment before the choice."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Commitment as Aftereffect Anchor.** An active behavioral step taken while under a pre-suasive influence "locks in" the resulting preference by shifting self-perception.
- **Three features that make a commitment maximally sticky:** active, effortful, voluntary.
- **Interior design of the mind.** Furnishing recurring daily spaces with cues that automatically route attention toward one's chosen directions. Not resisting automaticity — commissioning it.
- **If/when-then plans as daily cue triggers.** Chapter 9's tool, applied longitudinally.
- **Reminder signs as cue placement.** One carefully worded sign in the right physical location can outperform major interventions.
- **"Who we are is where we are, attentionally."** The book's one-sentence summary.

## Strategy 1 — Durable Change via Strong Commitments

### Medical Appointment Study (Steve Martin et al., British clinics)
- **Reminder calls** the day before appointments → 3.5% reduction in no-shows (costly, labor-intensive).
- **Patient fills in the appointment card themselves** (active commitment) → **18% reduction** in no-shows. Costless. ~$180M/year equivalent savings in UK alone.

### The 2008 US Flag Study
Pre-election online survey. Half the participants saw a small American flag in the corner; half did not. At the time, US flag was reliably associated with Republican Party thinking.
- Flag group: more favorable to McCain and conservative ideology immediately.
- Flag group: voted for McCain at significantly higher rates.
- **Eight months after the election**, flag-group participants still held more Republican-oriented attitudes.

Two mechanisms:
1. **Pre-suasion** — flag-prime temporarily channeled attention toward Republicanism.
2. **Commitment during that moment** — survey-takers acted on the inclination by registering it in the survey, and the vote followed; each committed step consolidated the attitude. Eight months later, the attitude held.

**Compare:** readers of a happy story rated a painting more favorably. Five days later, only those who had **actively rated** the painting while happy still preferred it. Those who had just felt the mood without making a behavioral commitment had no lingering preference.

### Three Sticky-Commitment Features
Commitments that reach into the future do so by changing self-perception. For that:
- **Active** — a behavior, not a thought or a passive agreement.
- **Effortful / Costly** — a sizable donation outperforms a token one.
- **Voluntary** — chosen freely, not coerced or pre-paid.

Each element says: *"This is who I am as a person."*

## Strategy 2 — Durable Change via Daily Cues (Cueing Up the Cues)

### The Yellow-and-Blue Bus Pyramid Scheme
Cialdini infiltrated a sales event where 50 people were bused from Phoenix to Tucson and back. Halfway, Cialdini realized another bus was going the opposite way carrying a different load — the event was *designed for the bus*, not the destination. Rolling, noisy, bumpy, emotionally agitating environment prevented hard thinking. Walls and seatbacks held achievement posters and wealth slogans; music was "Eye of the Tiger"; speakers wore tailored suits and waved commission checks. Two-thirds signed on by return. *"Modern life is becoming more and more like that bus."*

### The Response: Become the Interior Designer
Rather than fight automaticity, *commission* it. Furnish regularly-occupied spaces with cues that automatically route attention to the preferences you want. Earlier chapters showed how:
- **Writing for a general audience?** Surround the desk with faces of typical audience members (ch8).
- **Approaching a task that needs achievement orientation?** Images of success, striving, winning — the runner-winning-the-race photo (ch7, call-center lift of 60%).
- **Task needs analytical orientation?** Image of Rodin's *The Thinker* on the wall or desktop.
- **Changing orientation task-by-task?** Rotate computer desktop wallpaper to match.
- **Health goals?** If/when-then plans with real daily cues: "If/when after lunch the server asks about dessert, then I will order mint tea."

### The Hand-Washing Study (Grant & Hofmann)
Physicians wash their hands less than half as often as guidelines prescribe; interventions mostly fail. Two signs over soap/gel dispensers, one word different:
- **"Hand hygiene protects *you* from catching diseases."** → no effect.
- **"Hand hygiene protects *patients* from catching diseases."** → **+45% usage.**

Doctors *already* knew the link and cared about patients. The sign didn't inform — it *put the link at the top of mind* at the exact entry moment. Attention redirected = action redirected.

## The "Who Are Physicians Really?" Question

The hand-washing study suggests doctors are deeply other-oriented. But a Carnegie Mellon study on gift-taking from pharma complicates that.

**301 resident physicians asked whether taking industry gifts is acceptable:**
1. **Baseline ask:** 21.7% said acceptable.
2. **Preceded by questions about their personal/financial sacrifices to become MDs:** 47.5% said acceptable.
3. **Preceded by sacrifices questions AND framed as justified by their expended resources:** **60.3%** said acceptable.

The rule for reciprocation, surfaced pre-suasively (sacrifices made → returns owed), flipped the majority judgment on a serious professional-ethics question. The answer to "are MDs patient-serving or self-serving?" is: **yes, both — depending on their attentional focus at the time.**

This applies to all of us. The elasticity of preference is universal; MDs just illustrate it.

## Mental Models
- **Preference is a function of attention.** "Who we are with respect to any choice is where we are, attentionally, in the moment before the choice."
- **Three sources of what channels our attention:** haphazard environmental cues, cues placed by a knowing communicator, cues *we* have pre-placed in our own daily spaces. Only the last is ours.
- **Automaticity is not the enemy; it's the workforce.** Give it the right cues.
- **Commitment ≈ identity signing.** Active + effortful + voluntary = "this is me."
- **One sign in the right place > a campaign.** The hand-washing study shows the leverage.

## Anti-patterns
- **Expecting a pre-suasive shift to persist without a commitment or recurring cue.** It evaporates on the next attention switch.
- **Passive agreements.** "Yes, that sounds right" doesn't anchor.
- **Receptionist-filled appointment cards.** Lose the commitment effect. Hand the pen over.
- **Resisting automaticity by "trying harder."** Loses to the cue-laden environment. Redesign the environment instead.
- **Self-focused appeals to professionals who identify with service to others.** The "protect yourself" sign failed; "protect patients" won.
- **Reciprocity gifts to professionals you want to retain integrity.** Pharma gift-taking makes the point against — the pre-suasive invocation of sacrifice flips the ethical frame.

## Worked Example — The Appointment-Card Fix
Standard: receptionist writes next appointment time on card, hands to patient. No-shows ~X%. Intervention: receptionist puts the card in front of patient, asks them to write the time and date. No-show rate drops **18%**. Costless. Pure active commitment effect. Scale to the UK → ~$180M/year equivalent savings.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pre-suasion is temporary. To make it durable: commitment + recurring cues.
2. Active-effortful-voluntary commitments change self-perception; self-perception sticks.
3. A single behavior performed under a pre-suasive mood (writing on a card, filling in a survey, voting) can anchor the preference for months.
4. Design your daily environment as a cue system. Faces, images, slogans, music — place them where you'll pass them.
5. If/when-then plans are long-term cue triggers that you install for yourself.
6. "Who we are" at any decision = where our attention is in the moment before.
7. One well-placed sign at the point of action can outperform a whole intervention campaign.
8. The elasticity of preference is universal — not a flaw in physicians, but a feature of minds. Design for it.
