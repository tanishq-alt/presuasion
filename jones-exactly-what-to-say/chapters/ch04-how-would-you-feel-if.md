# Chapter 4: How Would You Feel If?

## Core Idea
Decisions are made on emotion first and justified with logic second. "How would you feel if…?" sends them forward in time to feel the emotion of the outcome — both the good and the bad — before the decision. The greater the contrast, the more likely they move.

## The Magic Words
> **"How would you feel if…?"**

## Why It Works
Jones's grounding:
- **Motivation = motive + action** = "a reason to move." Big-enough reasons move anyone.
- **Loss aversion**: people work *harder* to avoid a loss than to earn an equivalent gain.
- **Emotion first, logic second**: something has to feel right before it makes sense. Arguments that "make sense" but don't feel right don't move anyone.

"How would you feel if…?" installs a future emotional scene. Once they feel the scene, logic follows.

## When to Use
- Before asking for a decision, especially a stuck one.
- To create contrast between two futures (the one they want vs the one they're currently on track for).
- When your current pitch is "logically correct" but they're not budging.

## How
1. **Pair toward-and-away.** One question about a desired outcome, one about a loss. Contrast > either alone.
2. Make the scenario concrete (specific promotion, specific loss, specific name) — not abstract.
3. Stay silent after. Let them visit the feeling.

## Worked Examples
- "How would you feel if this decision led to your promotion?" (toward)
- "How would you feel if your competition passed you?" (away)
- "How would you feel if you turned this around?"
- "How would you feel if you lost everything?"
- Compound: "How would you feel if this time next year you were debt-free, living in your dream home, and planning your next vacation?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Vague futures** ("how would you feel if things got better?"). No image = no emotion.
- **All toward, no away** (or vice versa). You need the contrast; without it you're asking them to abandon the status quo for nothing in particular.
- **Fake stakes.** If the loss scenario is implausible they'll see the manipulation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Logic without emotion doesn't move people. Emotion without logic doesn't justify to themselves later. Use both — emotion first.
2. Loss is a bigger lever than gain. Use away motivation at least as much as toward.
3. Contrast is the mechanism — one future vs another, not one future vs nothing.
