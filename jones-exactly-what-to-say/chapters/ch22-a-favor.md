# Chapter 22: A Favor (The Referral Script)

## Core Idea
Ask for a favor *before* naming what it is. "Could you do me a small favor?" nearly always gets agreement before the person knows the ask. The worst answer is a conditional yes ("depends on what"). Best used right after they've said "thank you."

## The Magic Words
> **"Could you do me a small favor?"** / **"You couldn't do me a small favor, could you?"**

## Why It Works
- **Pre-commitment before disclosure.** Harder to back out once agreement is given.
- **"Thank you" = cue.** When they thank you, they feel a small debt. Gratitude is debt — ask right then.
- **"Small" shrinks the perceived cost.** Agreement is near-automatic.

## When to Use
- **Referral asks** (Jones's main case).
- Any time you want someone to agree to something before explaining what.
- Immediately after they thanked you for something.

## How: The Full Referral Script

**Step 1 — Cue.** They say "thank you" for something.

**Step 2 — The favor hook.**
> "You couldn't do me a small favor, could you?"

**Step 3 — The specific ask (verbatim-ish):**
> "You wouldn't happen to know **just one person** — someone who, **just like you**, would benefit from [the specific benefit they just thanked you for]?"

Why each bit:
- **"just one person"** — reasonable, easier to think of one name.
- **"just like you"** — subtly flatters, narrows to the right type.
- **"would benefit from [specific]"** — anchors on the thanked-for value, not generic selling.

**Step 4 — Shut up.** Silence. Let them think.

**Step 5 — When body language says they have someone, remove pressure:**
> "Don't worry — I'm not looking for their details right now, but who was it you were thinking of?"
(The "but" negates "don't worry" and they recall the name.)

**Step 6 — Find out when they'll next see this person.** Ask.

**Step 7 — Second favor ask (pre-commitment is already built):**
> "You couldn't do me a further favor, could you? Next time you see Steve, could you share a little bit about how it was doing business with me and see if he's perhaps open-minded about taking a phone call from me to see if I can help him in the same way I helped you?"

**Step 8 — Schedule the chase:**
> "Would it be okay if I gave you a call next week to find out how the chat with Steve went?"

**Step 9 — At the chase call, use ch07:**
> "I'm guessing you didn't get around to speaking to Steve?"

## Why the Slow Form Works
Slower process, faster outcome. You end up with qualified prospects who **expect your call**, **look forward to it**, and are **grateful**. Beats a name-and-number cold list every time.

## Anti-patterns
- **Asking for the referral *without* the favor preface.** Direct asks fail more often.
- **Big asks after "small favor."** Burns trust for next time.
- **Not cueing on thank-you.** Timing is 60% of the move.

## Key Takeaways
1. Thank-you is your referral trigger — install the reflex.
2. Pre-commitment (small favor → specific ask) outperforms direct asks consistently.
3. Qualified introductions beat cold leads; take the slower path.
