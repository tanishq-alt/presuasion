# Chapter 17: What Makes You Say That?

## Core Idea
Every objection is a transfer of control from you to them. The universal reversal: answer the objection with a question. "What makes you say that?" works on nearly every common objection and forces them to explain themselves — which usually weakens the objection.

## The Magic Words
> **"What makes you say that?"**

## Why It Works
- **Objection = shift of control.** When they raise one, you become obliged to respond to *them*. Flipping to a question flips control back.
- **Forces them to justify.** Many objections are reflexive, not reasoned. Asking for the reason exposes that.
- **Prevents prejudgment.** You now hear the *real* issue, not your assumption of it.
- **Avoids argument.** You can't argue when you're asking.

## When to Use
- Any common objection: "I need time," "I want to shop around," "I don't have money right now," "I need to speak to someone else," "wrong time."
- When your instinct is to deliver a counter-argument.
- Instead of trying to craft a unique response to every objection — this one works on most.

## How
1. Hear the objection fully.
2. Respond with exactly four words: "What makes you say that?"
3. Stay silent. Let them explain.
4. Based on what they actually say, pick your next move (often ch18 "before you make your mind up" or ch19 "if I can, will you").

## Worked Examples
- Customer: "I need to speak to somebody else before I make a decision." → You: "What makes you say that?"
- Customer: "Really, I don't have all the money right now." → You: "What makes you say that?"
- Customer: "I'm really not sure I've got the time to fit this in around what I'm doing right now." → You: "What makes you say that?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Sarcastic or challenging tone.** Now it's a trap.
- **Rushing to respond.** The silence after is where the magic happens.
- **Delivering a counter-argument first** then asking the question afterward. Loses the whole move.
- **Using it on the same objection twice.** Second time it feels evasive.

## Key Takeaways
1. The person asking the question controls the conversation.
2. Objections are often reflex — forcing explanation often dissolves them.
3. One four-word phrase handles the top five objections in sales.
