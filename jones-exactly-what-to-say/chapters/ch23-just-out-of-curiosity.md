# Chapter 23: Just Out of Curiosity

## Core Idea
Some questions are necessary but rude. "Just out of curiosity…" grants you permission to ask the rude question. It softens the preface without softening the question itself — giving you honest answers you otherwise can't get.

## The Magic Words
> **"Just out of curiosity…"**

## Why It Works
- **Permission by preface.** By labeling your motive as curiosity, you make the direct question feel exploratory rather than pushy.
- **Prompts honesty, not commitment.** You're not pushing for yes; you're asking what's really going on.
- **Silence afterward does the work.** The respondent either gives a real answer or realizes (during the long pause) they don't really have one.

## When to Use
- The classic stall: **"I need some time to think about it."** (Jones's primary case.)
- Any moment when the vague response leaves you nowhere.
- Close substitute when "what makes you say that?" (ch17) feels too confrontational.

## How
1. Deliver the preface: "Just out of curiosity…"
2. Ask the direct, almost-rude question.
3. **Shut up. Bite your tongue. Sit on your hands.**
4. Wait. Twelve seconds will feel like three weeks.
5. Two things can happen:
   - **~12 seconds:** They come back with a real honest answer. You can work with that.
   - **Longer:** The silence stretches. This is *good news* — they're hunting for an excuse and realizing they don't have one. They often say: "You know what, there isn't anything to think about."

## Worked Examples
- "Just out of curiosity, what is it specifically you need some time to think about?"
- "Just out of curiosity, what needs to happen for you to make a decision about this?"
- "Just out of curiosity, what is it that's stopping you from moving forward with this right now?"

## The Bigger Lesson
> *Asking big, brave questions is exactly what you need to do to become a professional mind-maker-upper.*

The seller who asks the question the prospect wasn't yet ready to ask themselves is the one who moves the decision. Most sellers avoid this question and lose the opportunity to a competitor who asks it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Filling the silence.** The single biggest failure mode. The silence *is* the lever.
- **Prejudging their answer.** Don't suggest reasons for them.
- **Delivering with aggression.** "Just out of curiosity" with a sarcastic edge is worse than asking directly.

## Key Takeaways
1. The preface grants permission to ask the question most sellers won't.
2. Silence is the operator; the question is just the setup.
3. If you don't ask the brave question, you lose to the person who does.
