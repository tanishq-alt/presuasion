# Chapter 10: Two Types of People

## Core Idea
"There are two types of people in this world…" prompts an instant internal question: *which one am I?* Make the choice you want the obvious one, and self-identification does the rest.

## The Magic Words
> **"There are two types of people in this world…"**

## Why It Works
- **Polarized choices are easy to make.** Red or white wine, beach or ski, binary beats broad.
- **Forced self-sort.** The listener's brain cannot help picking a side, and they want to be on the "better" one.
- **The sort is also the commitment** — once they've picked, backing off feels like changing who they are.

## When to Use
- Decision moments where the other person needs to self-identify to commit.
- Especially effective with people who respond to identity framing more than logic.
- As a close after the pitch has landed but momentum has stalled.

## How
1. State the two types as a bright-line split.
2. Stack the undesirable one as passive/nostalgic/cowardly without being insulting.
3. Stack the desirable one as agentic/courageous/forward-looking — and aligned with your offer.
4. Let the sentence land. Don't ask "which are you?" — the listener does that themselves.

## Worked Examples
- "There are two types of people in this world: those who leave their personal financial success in the hands of their employers, and those who take full responsibility and build their own futures."
- "There are two types of people in this world: those who judge something before they have even tried it, and those who are prepared to try something and base their opinion on their own experience."
- "There are two types of people in this world: those who resist change in favor of nostalgia, and those who move with the times and create a better future."
- Meta-example (Jones's closer for the book): "Those who read books like this and do nothing, and those who put what they read into practice and enjoy immediate results."

## Anti-patterns
- **Both options unappealing.** Nobody self-identifies, or they pick a third option you didn't offer.
- **Insulting the "wrong" side.** Makes the listener suspicious of the manipulation.
- **Asking "which are you?"** — the whole point is they ask themselves.

## Key Takeaways
1. Binary identity splits bypass analytical resistance.
2. Make the undesirable side feel passive or outdated, not stupid.
3. Self-identified commitment is stickier than argued-for agreement.
