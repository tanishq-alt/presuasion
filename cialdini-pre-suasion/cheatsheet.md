# Cheatsheet — Pre-Suasion

Decision rules and scenario-to-technique maps. Everything here should help you *pick the right move.*

---

## The One-Sentence Model

> **"Who we are with respect to any choice is where we are, attentionally, in the moment before the choice."**

Two consequences of channeled attention:
1. What's salient is treated as **important** (ch3).
2. What's focal is treated as **causal** (ch4).

Mental elements **fire when readied, not just when ready** (ch9).

| Situation | Move | Why |

| Asking for a favor | Prime "helpful / adventurous / open-minded" with a single-chute question | +40% compliance (ch2) |
| Writing to audience X | Work at the desk surrounded by their cues | Cialdini's two-desk discovery (ch8) |
| Giving a presentation | Open with a mystery; close with a surprising implication | Mysteries recruit explanation (ch6) |

| Choosing a metaphor | Pick one whose implied solution matches your preferred policy | 22% swing (ch7) |

| Getting referrals | See Jones ch22 — "Could you do me a small favor?" |

## The Seven Principles at a Glance

| Principle | Install by | Pre-suasive sharpener |
|---|---|---|
| **Reciprocation** | Giving first | Meaningful + unexpected + customized |
| **Liking** | Similarities + compliments | Show *you* like *them* |
| **Social Proof** | Peer behavior | Validity + feasibility |
| **Authority** | Expertise + trustworthiness | Weakness before strength + transition word |
| **Scarcity** | Loss framing | Purchase limits + time limits |
| **Consistency** | Small public step | Remind of it later |
| **Unity** | Shared identity "of us" | Kinship, place, language, synchrony, co-creation, advice-asking |

## Neidert's Stage-Principle Matrix

| Stage | Task | Primary principles |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make them favorable to **you** | Reciprocation, Liking |
| 2 | Make the decision feel **wise** | Social Proof, Authority |
| 3 | Make them **act** | Consistency, Scarcity |
| Throughout | Make it **durable** | Unity + commitment (ch14) |

## Trust Installation (Fast Path)

1. **Admit a genuine weakness first.**
2. **Use a transition word** — but / however / yet — that turns the weakness into a setup.
3. **Follow with a strength that *rebuts* the weakness** (not just adds a positive).
4. Example: *"I'm not experienced in this role, but I'm a very fast learner."*
5. Elizabeth I: *"Weak and feeble woman; but heart of a king."* (The heart rebuts the body's irrelevance.)

## Corrective Toolkit (When *You* Are the Target)

| Bias | Corrective |
|---|---|
| Single-chute survey question | Decline, or ask for the two-sided form |
| Sunny-day mood inflation | Interviewer asks about the weather first → bias disappears |
| Prominent product placement | More obvious → triggers your correction → *less* buying intent |
| Pyramid-scheme-bus / overloaded environment | Recognize: when rushed/tired/overloaded, you rely on cues; postpone the decision |
| Camera-angle bias (confession tape) | Ensure side-view angle or interrogator-focal angle |
| Your own ambient negativity | Lyubomirsky's three: morning gratitude list, cultivate optimism, negate the negative |

## The Integrity Guardrails

1. **Only use pre-suasion to amplify true cases.** False cases + pre-suasion compound the three tumors (perf / turnover / fraud) faster than the gain.
2. **Never fake the kinship / unity / authority / similarity cue.** Detection destroys the lever and everyone's trust in you.
3. **Measurement > exhortation** for installing ethical culture. Make it count toward compensation.
4. **Pre-suading yourself is the ethically cleanest practice ground.** Start there.
5. **If you'd feel exposed by a transparent third-party observer** reading the full transcript, don't do it.

## The If/When-Then Template

Form: **"If/when [specific cue I will encounter], then [specific action I will take]."**

| Goal | Template |
|---|---|
| Diet | "If/when after lunch the server asks about dessert, then I will order mint tea." |
| Medication | "When it is 8 AM and I finish brushing my teeth, then I will take my pill." |
| Productivity | "When I sit at my desk at 9 AM, then I will write for 25 minutes before opening email." |
| Chocolate temptation | "If/when I see chocolate displayed in the supermarket, then I will think of my diet." |

Rules:
1. Cue must be **specific and recurring**.
2. Action must be **specific and executable**.
3. Form the plan **in writing**, in the first person.
4. Pair with a **reminder sign** or environmental cue if the habit is new.

---

## Three Most Common Mistakes

1. **Working your way through merits of a case without first installing the pre-suasive frame.** You're pushing against a locked door. Install the opener first.
2. **Treating pre-suasion as a one-shot.** Without an immediate active commitment or daily cue environment, the shift evaporates.
3. **Applying pre-suasive techniques to false or coercive cases.** Short-term wins; triple-tumor compounding losses.

## The Operator's Checklist (Before Any Important Ask)

- [ ] What's my opener? What concept do I need their attention on in the moment before?
- [ ] Have I installed trust? (Weakness-first?)
- [ ] Which principle is this ask most naturally attached to? Have I surfaced it?
- [ ] Am I asking during a privileged moment or against one?
- [ ] After yes, do I have an active commitment ready for them to take?
- [ ] After today, what recurring cue will keep this top-of-mind?
- [ ] If an honest third party watched the whole exchange, would they approve?
