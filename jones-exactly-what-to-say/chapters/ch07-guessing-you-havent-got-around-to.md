# Chapter 7: I'm Guessing You Haven't Got Around To…

## Core Idea
When you're afraid someone hasn't done the thing they promised, don't ask if they did — assume they didn't, with phrasing that lets them save face. They'll either proudly correct you ("actually, I did") or sheepishly commit to doing it now.

## The Magic Words
> **"I'm guessing you haven't got around to…?"**

## Why It Works
- **Removes the usual excuses.** You've already stated the "I haven't done it" version — they can't use it as an out.
- **Face-saving.** The phrasing signals you expect the delay and aren't judging.
- **Forces one of two useful answers:** "No, I have — [progress]" or "No, you're right, I haven't — [new commitment]." Both move the conversation forward.

## When to Use
- Follow-ups where someone promised to speak to a partner, read a document, make a decision.
- Chasing without nagging.
- When you'd rather not open with "did you do the thing?" (invites bad news).

## How
1. State the specific uncompleted action directly in the question.
2. Deliver it as a gentle guess, not an accusation — tone matters more here than in most of the phrases.
3. Handle both outcomes:
   - "I have" → "Great — when are we ready to get started?"
   - "No, you're right" → open negotiation, ask what's blocking.

## Worked Examples
- "I'm guessing you haven't got around to speaking to your partner yet?"
- "I'm guessing you haven't got around to looking over the documents yet?"
- "I'm guessing you haven't got around to setting a date yet?"
- "I'm guessing you haven't got around to making a decision yet?"

## Anti-patterns
- **Accusatory tone.** Flips the move from face-saving to confrontational.
- **Vague object** ("haven't got around to it yet?"). Needs the specific thing.
- **Not having a response ready for both branches.** If they say "actually I have," you need to close on that momentum.

## Key Takeaways
1. People are mostly people of their word — being called out gently makes them rise, not retreat.
2. Push for the negative scenario to get them to the positive one.
3. Have the follow-up question ready for both possible answers.
