# Chapter 1: I'm Not Sure If It's for You, But…

## Core Idea
A rejection-free opener that lets you introduce anything to anyone. The phrase works because "I'm not sure if it's for you" lowers pressure while "but…" quietly negates the no-pressure framing so the listener actually leans in.

## The Magic Words
> **"I'm not sure if it's for you, but…"**

## Why It Works
- **No-pressure framing.** The subconscious hears "no pressure here" and drops its defenses.
- **Curiosity hook.** Suggesting they might not want it spikes "what is 'it'?"
- **"But" negates the preface.** As with "we love your work, *but* some things need to change" — the brain only remembers what comes after "but". The listener's inner voice hears: *you might want to look at this.*

## When to Use
- Introducing an unsolicited idea, product, or offer.
- Opening a cold conversation where rejection feels likely.
- Any time your first instinct is "I don't want to sound pushy."

## How
1. Say the preface verbatim — don't soften it further ("maybe I shouldn't bring this up but…"). The script *is* the softener.
2. Follow immediately with the thing. No pause, no apology.
3. Let them respond.

## Worked Examples (from the book)
- "I'm not sure if it's for you, but would you happen to know someone who's interested in [result of your product/service]?"
- "I'm not sure if it's for you, but we have plans on Saturday, and you're welcome to join us."
- "I'm not sure if it's for you, but this option is available for this month only, and I would hate for you to miss out."

## Expected Outcomes
Only two responses possible:
1. They lean in and ask for more because they're interested.
2. Worst case: "I'll think about it."

Either way you avoid a hard no.

## Anti-patterns
- **Softening the preface further** ("I know you're probably not interested but…"). Kills the curiosity.
- **Pitching before the preface.** Sequence matters — the preface has to arrive first.
- **Using it where there's no genuine fit.** Jones's integrity test: the result must hold up if an honest third party is watching.

## Key Takeaways
1. Rejection-free openers aren't passive — they shift the burden of curiosity onto the listener.
2. The word "but" is a pivot, not a disclaimer.
3. The worst realistic outcome is "I'll think about it," not a hard no.
