# Chapter 9: The Mechanics of Pre-Suasion — Causes, Constraints, and Correctives

## Core Idea
Mental elements don't just **fire when ready — they fire when *readied*.** After attention lands on an opener concept, closely linked concepts gain privileged accessibility in consciousness (and unlinked concepts get temporarily suppressed). That's the engine of pre-suasion. It's primitive (works on 18-month-olds), proportional to association strength, and its connections can even be *manufactured* from scratch (Pavlov).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Readied vs. ready.** Shakespeare: "The readiness is all." Readied = attention has activated a concept and its neighbors.
- **Accessibility accounting of pre-suasion.** Two simultaneous effects: linked concepts become more accessible; unlinked concepts become *less* accessible. Not addition only — also suppression.
- **If/when-then plans.** Pre-suasive self-statements of the form "If/when [specific cue], then [specific action]." They install a readied cue-→-action association ahead of time.
- **Mere reminders** as correctives. Simply directing attention to the *source* of bias usually cancels it.
- **Deliberative reasoning** as the slower corrective — but it's defeated by fatigue, time pressure, overload.
- **Hyperlink / prefetch metaphor for mental standby.** Some goals are pre-fetched — one reminder cue (one click) launches them.

## Three Questions About the Mechanism's Reach

### 1. How soon in life? — Very early.
Eighteen-month-old infants shown photos of two people standing *close together* were **3x more likely** to help the researcher pick up dropped items than toddlers shown two people apart or a single person. Togetherness and helpfulness are linked in the mind from pre-verbal age. The mechanism is foundational.

### 2. How far across association strength? — Proportional to association strength.
Cialdini's littering study. Four handbills left on windshields of a library parking lot, each promoting a different norm: (1) anti-littering (closest to the target behavior); (2) recycling (close); (3) turning off lights (moderate); (4) voting (far). A fifth, art-museum control. Owners' propensity to litter the handbill scaled directly with distance from the anti-littering norm. **The closer the opener's association to the desired behavior, the stronger the pre-suasive effect.**

Rule: an aspiring pre-suader who wants action X should find a concept strongly and positively associated with X and bring *that* to mind just before requesting X.

### 3. How manufacturable? — Fully.
No existing connection required. Pavlov had no natural bell-food link; repeated pairing made the bell ready the dog for eating. Advertising replicates this constantly: Tiger Woods + Buick; Beyoncé + Pepsi; Bob Dylan + Victoria's Secret. Research examples:
- Superimposing a Belgian beer brand five times on pictures of sailing, water-skiing, cuddling → more positive feelings toward the beer.
- A mouthwash brand on six beautiful nature scenes → more favorable immediately and still more favorable three weeks later.
- Subliminal eight-time exposure of thirsty people to happy faces (vs angry) before tasting a soft drink → drank more and were willing to pay 3x as much in a store. None of them knew they'd been influenced.

## If/When-Then Plans — The Operator's Tool

Intentions alone are weak. Translating intention into action succeeds only ~50% in health behaviors, undermined by (a) forgetting, (b) failing to recognize opportune moments, (c) distraction. If/when-then plans fix both:

**Form:** "If/when [specific cue encountered], then [specific action]."

**Examples with data:**
- *"If/when after my business lunch, the server asks if I'd like dessert, then I will order mint tea."* — dietary.
- *"When it is 8 AM and I finish brushing my teeth, then I will take my prescribed pill dose."* — epilepsy medication adherence rose from **55% to 79%**.
- *"If/when lunch is over and space has become available at the lunchroom table, then I will start writing my employment history there."* — hospitalized opiate addicts in withdrawal: **0% of control group** turned in the history by day's end; **80% of the if/when-then group** did.
- *"If/when I see chocolate displayed in the supermarket, then I will think of my diet."* — chronically unsuccessful dieters ate fewer high-calorie foods and lost more weight.

**Why it beats "I intend to…" / "I plan to…":** the if/when-then form (a) sets high alert for a specific cue, (b) binds the cue directly to the desired action. You get to install heightened vigilance and a pre-built response in yourself. Pre-suasion on self, with self-chosen trigger.

**Hyperlink framing:** major goals are prefetched. One well-chosen cue can launch them. If/when-then plans design the cue.

## Correctives — Can We Resist Being Manipulated?

### Simple reminders usually work
- **Weather bias:** on sunny days people rate their own lives 20% more satisfying than on rainy days. But if the interviewer first asks, *"By the way, how's the weather over there?"* — the bias vanishes entirely.
- **Flirtation success** on the street: man complimenting women and asking for phone numbers succeeded 22.4% on sunny mornings vs 13.9% on cloudy ones.

### The product-placement finding that undercuts conventional wisdom
*Seinfeld* study. Prominent product placements (name shown AND spoken) produced more recognition and recall — standard industry metrics. But a third measure — **"which would you actually buy?"** — reversed the finding. The most subtly placed brands were chosen by **47%**; the most prominently placed only **27%**. Prominence cued viewers to the advertiser's trick, triggering a correction *against* the bias. Subtle insertions won.

### To "re-mind" is literally to put back in mind
Reminders work because they put already-held knowledge back on the top of the stack at the moment of judgment. Minimal, short, well-timed. Reminder = attention redirect at the moment of bias.

## Correctives Fail Under Load
Deep analysis is the second corrective — but it's expensive. When people are:
- **Rushed** (camera study: with 2 sec per feature, only 17% picked the better product; with 5 sec, 38%; unlimited time, 67% chose correctly).
- **Fatigued** (infomercials sell better late at night; combat units begin shelling civilian targets without question after 24–36 sleepless hours; false confessions average **16-hour** interrogations vs 1-hour normal interrogations).
- **Overloaded, preoccupied, indifferent, stressed, distracted, or a conspiracy theorist.**

In modern life all of these conditions are the baseline. Deep analysis "rarely arrives because it's rarely summoned." So pre-suasion operators can expect the automatic machinery to dominate.

## Mental Models
- **Readiness = accessibility + suppression.** Firing one concept dims its competitors.
- **Hyperlink/prefetch.** Most major goals are a single-click launch. If/when-then plans design the click.
- **Mind the gap.** Between stimulus and response is a brief window for correction. Reminders reopen that window.
- **To remind = to put back in mind.** Don't underestimate the strength of pointing at the obvious.
- **Automaticity dominates unless summoned otherwise.** Deliberation is the fallback corrective, costly and often skipped.

## Anti-patterns
- **"I plan to lose weight."** No cue, no action, no readied link. Low success.
- **Blatant product placements in your own content.** Too obvious → triggers correction → backfires on purchase intent even while winning on recall.
- **Big decisions at night, tired, or rushed.** Automatic machinery wins; corrective fails.
- **Assuming good intentions will convert to action.** Half the time they don't. Install an if/when-then.
- **Using a weakly-associated opener.** If the association is distant, the effect is weak.

## Worked Example — If/When-Then for Opiate Addicts
Hospitalized opiate-withdrawal patients were asked to produce an employment history by day's end to help with post-release jobs. **Control group:** 0/N completed by day's end. **If/when-then group** (asked to form the plan "If/when lunch is over and space has become available at the table, then I will start writing my employment history there"): **80%** turned in completed résumés. The plan did the work the intention couldn't.

## Key Takeaways
1. Fire when readied, not just when ready. Pre-suaders design the readying.
2. Accessibility works both ways: fire the concept you want, suppress the ones you don't.
3. The infant data (18 months) shows the machinery is foundational, not learned.
4. If the ideal association already exists, use it. If not, manufacture one.
5. If/when-then plans turn intentions into cue-triggered actions; they consistently beat intentions and generic action plans.
6. Reminders are cheap and often sufficient correctives. Use them against your own biases.
7. When rushed, tired, or overloaded, deliberation disappears. Choose time and context for important decisions deliberately.
