# Chapter 21: Just One More Thing (The Columbo Move)

## Core Idea
Don't leave empty-handed. When the main close has failed and you're walking out, turn around and say "Just one more thing…" — their guard is down because they think you're done. Now ask for something smaller.

## The Magic Words
> **"Just one more thing…"**

## Why It Works
- **Guard is down.** They've relaxed because the pressure's over.
- **"Downsell" instead of upsell.** If the big ask fails, a small ask succeeds more often than a repeat of the big ask.
- **Columbo precedent.** The TV detective famously got the real answer with this exact move — the suspect thought the interrogation was over.
- **Leaving with something > leaving with nothing.** A sample, a small order, an event invite, or a referral keeps the relationship alive.

## When to Use
- End of a meeting that didn't close.
- As you literally pack up and head for the door.
- When the big offer failed but a small one would be a viable first step.

## How
1. Do a visible "end" — stand up, pick up your bag, start to leave.
2. At the moment they think the conversation is over, turn back.
3. Point your finger lightly, deliver: "Just one more thing."
4. Ask for the smaller thing — not a repeat of the big ask.

## Downsell Options
- Ask them to **sample a product** (lowest commitment).
- Ask them to commit to a **small order** / trial.
- Invite them to an **event**.
- Introduce them to **someone you think they should know**.
- Ask them to **do something for you** (favor-ask, ch22).
- Ask a **scarcity question** about your first offer ("just curious — if the price were X, would that change things?").

## Worked Example
> You've pitched; they're not committing. You thank them for the time, pack up, head for the door. At the door, turn back:
> "Just one more thing — would you mind if I sent you a sample so you can see for yourself?"
> They nearly always say yes.

## Anti-patterns
- **Repeating the main ask.** Not what the move is for; feels desperate.
- **Faking the departure.** The move only works if your end-of-meeting signals are real.
- **Multiple "just one more things."** It's the Columbo *moment*, not the Columbo *speech*.

## Key Takeaways
1. The smaller ask after the main ask succeeds far more often than retrying the main ask.
2. Guard-down moments produce easier yeses — exploit them legitimately.
3. Never leave a meeting with nothing. A sample is better than a goodbye.
