# Patterns — Pre-Suasion

Every pattern below is a usable pre-suasive technique: **when to use, how to install, trade-offs.**

---

## OPENERS AND FRAMES

### 1. The Anchor Opener
**When:** before stating a price, number, or magnitude the target might contest.
**How:** mention a large reference number (even humorously unrealistic) immediately before your real number.
**Example:** "I'm not going to be able to charge you a million dollars for this. [pause] The fee is $75,000."
**Trade-off:** eliminates pushback almost every time; doesn't always win business.

### 2. The Trust Opener (Jim the Fire-Alarm Salesman)
**When:** at entry, when you need the prospect to associate you with trusted-insider status.
**How:** arrange to be treated in a way a trusted person would be (handed a key, left alone briefly).
**Trade-off:** must be genuine / plausible; contrivance backfires.

### 3. The Single-Chute Question (Ethical Caveat Required)
**When:** before a request where you want the respondent to see themselves as the kind of person who'd grant it.
**How:** ask "Do you consider yourself [adventurous / helpful / open-minded]?" — then make the ask.
**Trade-off:** Compliance ~doubles; ethically only usable when the respondent is actually the kind of person you're asking them to confirm. Cult recruiters abuse this.

### 4. Background Cue Design
**When:** before any decision environment you control.
**How:** place subtle cues aligned with the preference you want. Clouds → comfort / sofa; pennies → price / cheap sofa. Runner-winning-race photo → achievement-orientation and +60% productivity.
**Trade-off:** denied / unrecognized by subjects even after disclosure; extremely durable effect.

### 5. Weakness-Before-Strength (Trust Installer)
**When:** when your audience is wary or you need trust fast.
**How:** mention a genuine weakness first, then pivot with "but/however/yet" to a counterbalancing strength that *undercuts the weakness's relevance.*
**Example (Elizabeth I at Tilbury):** "I know I have the body of a weak and feeble woman; **but** I have the heart of a king, and a king of England, too."
**Trade-off:** the weakness must be real and non-fatal; the pivot must genuinely rebut.

### 6. The Family Frame
**When:** when stakeholders are nervous about trusting your continuing guidance.
**How:** in writing or speech, explicitly state "I will tell you what I would tell my family." (Buffett 2015 letter.)
**Trade-off:** requires prior demonstrated honesty; contrived use burns the lever.

## ATTENTION CAPTURE

### 7. Mystery-Story Teaching (Six Steps)
**When:** opening a lecture, presentation, article, or case study.
**How:**
1. Pose the mystery (surprising fact).
2. Deepen it (another surprising fact that compounds).
3. Consider and eliminate alternative explanations.
4. Provide a clue.
5. Resolve the mystery.
6. Draw the implication for the broader teaching point.
**Trade-off:** more preparation than straight exposition; dramatically higher retention and understanding.

### 8. The Cliffhanger Writing Habit
**When:** writing projects you want to return to.
**How:** never finish a session at the end of a paragraph or complete thought. Stop mid-paragraph with the next sentence pre-known but unwritten.
**Trade-off:** small discipline; large compounding productivity.

### 9. "What questions do you have for me?" (Jones Simple Swap)
**When:** end of every pitch, presentation, demo.
**How:** replace "Any questions?" with "What questions do you have for me?"
**Trade-off:** either you get specific objections or you get "none" = close signal.

### 10. The Decoy-by-Contrast (Dream/Titan)
**When:** your product beats a direct rival on one feature, loses on another.
**How:** add 2-3 weaker options that share the *rival's* strength. Your product now stands out as the uniquely-feature option.
**Trade-off:** requires catalog breadth; works even when you lose on the direct comparison.

### 11. Managed Video Cuts
**When:** producing persuasive video.
**How:** use cuts sparingly, each one to redirect attention to your strongest feature. Don't cut for stimulation.
**Trade-off:** over-cutting raises total attention but *reduces* persuasion and memory.

## MOVING PEOPLE WITH ASSOCIATIONS

### 12. Metaphor Selection (Frame + Solution)
**When:** describing a problem you want solved a particular way.
**How:** pick a metaphor whose implied solution matches your preferred solution. Crime-as-beast → catch/cage. Crime-as-virus → remove conditions.
**Trade-off:** one word can shift preferred policy by 22% — more than gender or party.

### 13. Non-Verbal Metaphor Transfer
**When:** evaluating candidates or ideas.
**How:** hand a heavy clipboard (seriousness transfer); hand a warm cup before the meeting (trust/warmth transfer).
**Trade-off:** small but reliable; works even when subjects deny the effect.

### 14. The "You" Swap in Copy
**When:** ad copy, email subject lines, presentations.
**How:** replace "people/they" with "you." (Ohio State study.)
**Trade-off:** magnifies whatever follows — strengthens strong cases, worsens weak ones.

### 15. Implicit Egoism Hooks (Self-Markers)
**When:** low-stakes initial engagement, brand attachment.
**How:** share or surface birthday / name / initials / birthplace matches with the target. Coca-Cola first-name campaign; microfinance initial-matching.
**Trade-off:** very small per-encounter effect; extraordinary at scale.

### 16. Rhyme / Fluency Upgrade
**When:** writing claims, slogans, memorable principles.
**How:** rhyme what you want believed. "To make it climb, make it rhyme."
**Trade-off:** judged more true; limited to short claims.

## GEOGRAPHIES

### 17. Workspace-Audience Matching
**When:** writing or working for a specific audience.
**How:** structure the environment with cues of the audience (faces, publications they read, items they use). Cialdini wrote his popular book from home, not his university office.
**Trade-off:** requires mental time to identify the right cue-load.

### 18. The Photo-Poster Workaround
**When:** when you can't choose your work environment (client-controlled).
**How:** bring portable cues — enlarged photos of the audience, posters, objects — and set them around the available room.
**Trade-off:** clients tend to read this as "personalized touch"; genuinely useful for the team.

### 19. Self-Affirmation Pre-Test
**When:** before someone enters a stereotype-threat environment.
**How:** have them write briefly about a personal value (friendship, family) and why it matters to them.
**Trade-off:** one 10-min exercise, done twice in a semester, raised women's physics-exam grades by a full letter.

### 20. Stereotype-Threat Removal
**When:** administering a test or interview to a stereotype-threatened group.
**How:** (a) don't ask for the stereotype-relevant demographic at the start; (b) replace it with an accomplishment demographic ("year in school"); (c) assign counter-stereotype role models as proctors; (d) pre-test self-affirmation.
**Trade-off:** combined, these *eliminate* measurable stereotype-threat performance gaps.

### 21. Attentional Happiness Routine (Lyubomirsky)
**When:** daily, to run counter to negative loops.
**How:** morning gratitude list (written); pre-decide to look on the bright side; limit time dwelling on problems and unhealthy social comparisons.
**Trade-off:** daily discipline required — like going to the gym.

## THE SEVEN PRINCIPLES OPERATIONALIZED

### 22. Reciprocation — Gift First, Triple-Multiplied
**When:** initial interaction, opening of a business relationship.
**How:** give first with a gift that is **meaningful** (not necessarily expensive), **unexpected** (not expected as part of the transaction), **customized** (fits the recipient's specific context). Example: sugar-free cookies for the diabetic prisoner.
**Trade-off:** supplying resources up front without guaranteed return can misfire; the three multipliers reduce misfire risk sharply.

### 23. Liking — "Show you like them" (not "get them to like you")
**When:** sales and relationship situations.
**How:** highlight genuine similarities; offer genuine, specific compliments. Mirror verbal style and non-verbal posture (ethically).
**Trade-off:** flattery with ulterior motive still works but burns on detection; prefer genuine.

### 24. Social Proof — Validity + Feasibility
**When:** motivating behavior change (environmental, health, purchase).
**How:** communicate that similar others are doing it (validity) *and* implicitly that it's achievable (feasibility). The Cialdini energy-conservation message outperformed save-money messaging by 3.5x.
**Trade-off:** must be true social proof.

### 25. Authority — Install Trust with a Weakness
**When:** when you need to be believed quickly and credibly.
**How:** present yourself/your offer by first acknowledging a genuine weakness, then pivoting with a transition word to a strength that undercuts the weakness.
**Trade-off:** the weakness must be real; trust comes only from the acknowledgment pattern, not from the content of the weakness.

### 26. Scarcity — Frame Loss, Not Gain
**When:** closing, upgrading, moving quickly.
**How:** frame the alternative-to-action as *loss* ("you'll lose $20K if you don't act now"), not as *missed gain.* Impose purchase limits when genuine.
**Trade-off:** manufactured scarcity burns trust when detected.

### 27. Consistency — Small Public Step First, Then Scale
**When:** motivating action from someone who has previously expressed some alignment.
**How:** install a small active, public, voluntary step; later remind them of it when asking for the larger congruent step. Honesty pledge at the *start* of an odometer form; patient writes next appointment on the card.
**Trade-off:** the small step must be real (not coerced); the remind step must feel natural.

### 28. Unity — "Of us, not like us"
**When:** across all stages, but especially for durable loyalty.
**How:** invoke shared identity — kinship (real or fictive), place (home / locality / region), language (speak theirs), synchrony (music, marching), co-creation, advice-asking.
**Trade-off:** cheap or inauthentic use of "we / family / brotherhood" labels burns the lever; genuine connection compounds.

## UNITY TACTICS

### 29. Shared-Language Signal (Hostage / Negotiation)
**When:** cross-cultural negotiation where group identity matters.
**How:** switch to a negotiator who genuinely speaks the counterpart's language, even if imprecisely. Kim Man-bok and Pashtun; the Taliban released the hostages.
**Trade-off:** must be real competence; interpreters can't substitute.

### 30. The 97% Favor — Kinship Linkage
**When:** mass-request to a group where recipients have kin connections.
**How:** route the request through kin. Cialdini's parent survey (one test point for the student) got 97% response.
**Trade-off:** one-time; scaled abuse would erode the lever.

### 31. Advice-Giving Mode Switch
**When:** you want someone to feel bonded to your brand / project / cause.
**How:** ask for "advice." Do *not* ask for "opinions" or "expectations." Splash! restaurant study: advice mode → significantly higher patronage intent through felt brand linkage.
**Trade-off:** must genuinely want and use the advice; perfunctory requests detect-and-fail.

### 32. The Columbo Move (Jones Cross-Reference)
**When:** walking out of a failed close. See Jones skill ch21. Pair with a unity-building downsell.

### 33. Synchrony Prime
**When:** need team cooperation or cross-group bias reduction.
**How:** have the group walk in step, clap in rhythm, sing together, or engage in a short synchronized physical activity. Teams that marched in step cooperated 50% more.
**Trade-off:** transparent as a tool; still works. The experience is pleasant, which protects the lever.

### 34. Reciprocal Self-Disclosure (Arons' 36 Questions)
**When:** rapid relationship formation.
**How:** paired turn-taking with escalating-disclosure questions. 45 minutes produces love-depth bonds.
**Trade-off:** requires both parties to open proportionally; forced use is manipulative.

## IF/WHEN-THEN PLANS

### 35. Self-Installed Cue → Action Links
**When:** any health or productivity goal you've struggled to automate.
**How:** form "If/when [specific recurring cue], then [specific action]" — not "I intend to."
**Examples:**
- "If/when after my business lunch, the server asks about dessert, then I will order mint tea."
- "When it is 8 AM and I finish brushing my teeth, then I will take my pill."
- "If/when lunch is over and the lunchroom table has space, then I will start writing my employment history."
**Trade-off:** specificity is the whole lever. "If I'm hungry, then I'll eat healthy" is too vague to work.

### 36. Reminder-Sign as Intervention
**When:** a known-but-unprioritized behavior underperforms.
**How:** place one short, correctly-framed sign at the point of action. Hand-hygiene: *"protects *patients*"* produced +45% soap use; *"protects *you*"* produced 0%.
**Trade-off:** framing matters as much as placement. Audience-appropriate framing required.

### 37. The Check-Your-Weather Corrective
**When:** making a judgment while in a mood-inflating state (sunny day, happy story).
**How:** explicitly ask / have the respondent ask themselves "what's influencing me right now?" Cancels bias.
**Trade-off:** requires either the bias-aware operator or an aware target. Won't fire when neither side notices.

## COMMITMENT AND DURABILITY (POST-SUASION)

### 38. The Patient-Fills-The-Card
**When:** booking any future commitment.
**How:** have the committed party actually write/sign the detail themselves. No-show rate drops ~18%.
**Trade-off:** minimal effort; large durable gain.

### 39. The Pre-Election Flag (and its generalization)
**When:** reinforcing an attitude change you want to persist.
**How:** pair the pre-suasive opener with an immediate active commitment (fill in a survey, cast a vote, send an email, share a quote). The active step locks the shift.
**Trade-off:** must be an authentic step on the part of the target; coerced steps don't anchor.

### 40. Measurement of Ethics (Compensation Loop)
**When:** installing a durable ethical culture.
**How:** (a) customer-honesty ratings of employees feed employee incentives; (b) firm-wide ethical reputation feeds annual-performance assessments; (c) employee-ratings of leadership ethics feed CEO / senior-management compensation.
**Trade-off:** culturally expensive to install; produces measurement-driven attention on ethics, which is the lever.

## CHAINED SEQUENCES

### A. Cold → Warm → Committed
1. **Opener** — the weakness-first or family frame (ch10, ch11).
2. **Attention** — mystery-story setup (ch6) or vivid metaphor (ch7).
3. **Principle** — reciprocation + liking (stage 1 of Neidert).
4. **Uncertainty reduction** — social proof + authority (stage 2).
5. **Action** — consistency + scarcity (stage 3) + an active commitment step (ch14).

### B. Difficult Conversation (negotiation, raise request, hard ask)
1. **Pre-suasive opener** with weakness-first trust installer.
2. **Metaphor** reframing the situation your way.
3. **Ask for advice** (unity mode switch).
4. **Explicit "If I can [do X], will you [do Y]?"** (if/when-then conditional close — see Jones ch19).

### C. Writing a Document to Persuade
1. Write at the desk where the audience lives (ch8).
2. Open with a mystery (ch6).
3. State weakness/counter-argument before claim (ch10).
4. Use rhyme, concrete metaphor, and "you"-framing (ch7).
5. Close with a trivial easy commitment step (ch14).

### D. Building a Durable Habit in Yourself
1. Pick the behavior.
2. Place a reminder sign at the point of action with correct audience framing.
3. Write an if/when-then plan.
4. Record the first execution as a deliberate active commitment.
5. Furnish the recurring environment with success/achievement cues (ch7, ch8).

## INTEGRITY RULES (CH13)

1. Only apply pre-suasion to *true* cases. Applied to lies, the three tumors (performance, turnover, fraud) compound faster than the gain.
2. Never fake similarity, kinship, authority, or trust. Fake trust burns the ability to borrow trust in the future.
3. Mandatory truthfulness about weaknesses when using the weakness-first install.
4. Prefer measurement-based ethics over moral exhortation.
5. **Who we are with respect to any choice is where we are, attentionally, in the moment before the choice.** The ethical operator's responsibility is to place genuine, truthful cues there.
