# Chapter 13: Don't Worry

## Core Idea
Two words, said calmly, can defuse an anxious prospect faster than any argument. "Don't worry" gives permission for the tension to drop and signals that *you've got this*.

## The Magic Words
> **"Don't worry…"**

## Why It Works
- **Immediate tonal reset.** Anxiety is a physical state — a confident "don't worry" is a cue to exhale.
- **Repositions you as the one in control.** When someone is unsure what to do next, they want a calm voice to follow.
- **Opens the door to leading.** After "don't worry," they can hear instructions they couldn't hear through the panic.

## When to Use
- Visible nerves or hesitation ("I'm not sure what to do…").
- High-stakes decision moments (big purchase, commitment).
- Just after someone has raised a scared objection.
- Right before narrating "what happens next" (ch16).

## How
1. Deliver calmly. Slow down. Lower your tone.
2. Keep your posture relaxed and open — they're reading your body before your words.
3. **Follow it with a lead.** "Don't worry" alone leaves them in the panic; add the next step.
4. Normalize their feeling ("you're bound to be nervous right now") before pivoting to action.

## Worked Examples
- "Don't worry. You're bound to be nervous right now."
- "Don't worry — I know you don't know what to do right now, but that's what I'm here for. I'm here to help you through this process and overcome all the hurdles as they crop up."
- "Don't worry. I felt just the way you feel right now before I started, and look at me now."

## Anti-patterns
- **Delivered fast or dismissively.** Now you sound patronizing.
- **"Don't worry" without a follow-up lead.** They're still worried; they needed direction.
- **Using it when the concern is legitimate and warrants engagement.** Sometimes they need you to *address* the worry, not wave it away.

## Key Takeaways
1. Calm is contagious — your tone is the delivery mechanism, not the words.
2. Normalize, then lead. Never leave them alone in the anxiety.
3. Two words, used sparingly, are more powerful than a paragraph of reassurance.
