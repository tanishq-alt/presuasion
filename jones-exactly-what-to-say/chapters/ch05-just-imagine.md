# Chapter 5: Just Imagine…

## Core Idea
Every decision is made twice — first in imagination, then in reality. If someone can't see themselves doing it, they won't. "Just imagine…" opens their mental image viewer and lets *them* paint the scene, which they'll trust more than any picture you paint.

## The Magic Words
> **"Just imagine…"**

## Why It Works
- Adults can't be opened with "once upon a time," but "just imagine" does the same job: subconscious switches on the image viewer and starts picturing the scenario you name.
- Their own imagination is more vivid and more trusted than any sales story you could tell.
- Once they've seen the outcome, belief in it rises sharply.

## When to Use
- Right before asking for commitment.
- After "how would you feel if…?" has planted the emotion and you want to make the scene vivid.
- To get past "I can't see it" / "I don't know how it would work" objections.

## How
1. Pick a sensory, concrete scene — a visible moment, not an abstraction.
2. Apply the same toward/away split from ch04: imagine the good outcome, or the cost of the opposite.
3. Hold a beat after the sentence; let them watch the movie.

## Worked Examples
- "Just imagine how things will be in six months once you have implemented this."
- "Just imagine what your boss would say if you missed this opportunity."
- "Just imagine the look on your kids' faces when they see you achieve this."
- "Just imagine stepping up on stage and picking up that big incentive check."
- "Just imagine pulling into the driveway in your brand-new car."

## Anti-patterns
- **Over-direction** ("Just imagine the sunlight on the oak floors of the new kitchen you designed in Sedona…"). Too specific, no room for them to project. Give a scene scaffold, not a painting.
- **Negative images at the wrong moment.** Use away-motivation imagery earlier in the conversation, not right before asking for the close.
- **Stacking multiple "just imagines" back to back.** Dilutes each one.

## Key Takeaways
1. People follow their own pictures, not yours. Hand them the brush.
2. Decision in the mind precedes decision in reality — that's not a metaphor, it's the mechanism.
3. One vivid "just imagine" beats three logical arguments.
